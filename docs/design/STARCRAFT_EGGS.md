# StarCraft homage eggs

Plan only. Nothing in this note is in the game. No code, no new enemy, no new cinematic slide, and no cheat item ships with this page.

Unsubscribe 2087 already has its invasions. They are corporate. Phonix left. MANDATE stayed. Nobody in Stillwater has met a thing from off the planet, and the hero knows that. The jokes below lean on that. A famous real-time strategy game is the reference. The lines, names, and pictures in the game would be ours.

## Rules

- Parody names only. The table below is the allow-list for anything a player can read.
- No studio assets: no logos, no unit renders, no icons traced off a screenshot, no voice clips, no music cues.
- A silhouette, if one is ever drawn, is a new scribble: low, many-legged, wrong for this mall. It is not a copy of a unit.
- The shipped sentence is the 2087 line in each idea. The famous line is named so we know what we are twisting. It is not pasted onto a button.
- One bark, once. These are eggs. They do not become a faction.

Effort is S (a line or one slide), M (a person, a slide with a drawing, or a small item), or L (a new fight). IP risk is Low when the line is ours, Medium when a player will still hear the original in their head, High if we would need their art, their audio, or their exact sentence on screen. High is out.

## 1. The Nipper crosses a cinematic

Micah's idea. During a cinematic a Nipper runs through the shot. The hero is confused. As far as they know, there has not been an alien invasion. There will not be one. The creature is lost.

**Placement.** One extra slide in the Act I opening, on the cut into the Parcel Moors, before Gatewarden HOA-9. The lawns are four centimetres. Something lower than the grass crosses left to right and is gone. Gramps, if he is in the slide, does not look up.

**2087 twist.** The hero: "That was not a drone." Gramps: "Dog." The hero: "Dogs do not have that many opinions." Gramps: "Then it was somebody's visor, and it got out. Keep walking. The invasion already happened. It had a terms of service."

**Effort.** M. One slide of text is S. A scribble of the Nipper, original, makes it M. No new AI, no spawn, no loot.

**IP risk.** Low if the scribble is ours and the slide never names a faction. Medium if the drawing starts to match a unit everybody recognises. Stop at "too many legs, wrong mall."

## 2. You must subscribe additional relays

The power-building line, said when a thing the player expected to work has no socket left.

**Placement.** Two existing failures, not a new screen. Casting with an empty energy bar already says "Not enough energy." A Founder at the intern cap already refuses another summon. Add the relay line as the second sentence, once per town visit, on the first of those failures. Stillwater Commons is enough for the player to meet it. It can repeat in the Updraft, where the floor is already a machine that needs power.

**2087 twist.** "You must subscribe additional relays." Then, smaller: "The free tier covers four." Energy and headcount were the pylons all along. Nobody constructs anything. They accept the next plan.

**Effort.** S.

**IP risk.** Low. The verb is the joke. The original sentence does not appear.

## 3. Need more sats. Not enough vapour.

The two resource barks, tied to money we already have.

**Placement.** Any spend that already fails. Brenda, Nonce, Kestrel's hire, a shrine that costs sats, the lockbox fee. The first failure in a visit says "Need more sats." A second, different failure in the same visit says "Not enough vapour." Vapour is not a new currency. The line means the Stipend's green remainder, the part of the price that is not the coin. The Data Lake and the Boardwalk are the meanest places to hear the second line, because nothing there has a price tag until it does.

**2087 twist.** Minerals were the honest rock. Sats already are that, in this game. Vapour is the fee beside them: adoption, social credit, the spread. "Need more sats" is the bitcoiner bark. "Not enough vapour" is Unity, charging for the smell of the trade. Mr. Float can say the second one when a loan is short. He would.

**Effort.** S. Two toasts, a per-visit latch so they do not spam.

**IP risk.** Low. We never say the gas's name.

## 4. Reyn, late of the watch

A mercenary in the Jim Raynor shape: a decent person who used to wear a uniform, still wears the coat, and hires out because the coat's employer went bad.

**Placement.** Kestrel's row in Stillwater Commons, as a second hire beside the existing companions, or a one-off bodyguard for the Glass Cathedral approach who will not enter the building. He has already been inside once. He talks about the Regional Office the way a person talks about a commander they stopped saluting.

**2087 twist.** His name is Reyn. He was Parcel Watch until the watch started fining people for the length of their grief. He will fight HOA machines and he will not fight strikers. His hire line: "I used to be the marine. Then the marine got a visor. The visor is still employed." He costs credits, not a speech. If he dies he stays dead until Kestrel, same as the others. He does not get a portrait from anywhere else.

**Effort.** M. A hire row, a bark, no new combat role. L if he gets his own skill.

**IP risk.** Medium. A gravel-voiced ex-soldier is a wide trope. Keep him a fired watchman with a road-sign opinion, not a marshal with a ship. No cigar, no badge, no voice.

## 5. Spawn more barges

The supply line, said when the player is carrying more fight than they have pockets for.

**Placement.** The Founder, at the summon cap, in any district. The first refusal each visit. The Mezzanine is the right cruel room for it, because Vale's floor is already full of things that were sent up. A second, quieter use: the belt, when stims are at cap and the pack just took the overflow. "Spawn more barges" meaning "your pockets are the barge."

**2087 twist.** "Spawn more barges." The Founder hears it as headcount. Everyone else hears it as inventory. The barge is not a creature. It is the next subscription tier of carrying things. Interns are not larvae. Do not draw a floating sac.

**Effort.** S for the line. L if a barge is ever a unit. Do not make it a unit.

**IP risk.** Low for the line. High if we draw the sac. We will not.

## 6. En tariff

A greeting that is almost a prayer, said by one person who thinks it is ordinary.

**Placement.** One Stillwater local, not Gramps and not Brenda. A new idling line on an existing chatterer in the commons, or a single node-tender near Nonce if he is in town. They say it when the hero walks up. They do not explain it. If the hero's lean is bitcoiner they add "executor" and they are wrong about the title. If the lean is fiat they say it the same way anyway, which is the joke.

**2087 twist.** "En tariff." Not a blessing. A hope that the fee falls on someone else. The second line, once, if the player waits: "En tariff, and mind the spread." No golden armor. No third eye. The speaker is a person in a coat who read one old manual and kept the hello.

**Effort.** S.

**IP risk.** Medium if we complete the famous couplet. Low if we stop at "En tariff" and never add the name that follows it in the original. Stop there.

## 7. The Queen of Margins

The bladed-queen allusion, aimed at a woman this story already has, without turning her into someone else.

**Placement.** A codex memo on the Boardwalk or the Mezzanine, filed next to Vale's internal profile. Not a boss rename. Vale stays Vale. Phonix stays Phonix. The memo is someone else's gossip.

**2087 twist.** "They promoted her once, and the promotion grew edges. People who liked the first version still ask for it. The version that remained is better at the job and worse at being asked." The queen is a job title. The blades are the terms. Nobody sprouts wings. A player who knows the reference will wince. A player who does not will think it is about Vale, which it is.

**Effort.** S. One memo.

**IP risk.** Medium. The risk is finishing the allusion out loud: a name, a swarm, a transformation scene. The memo never does that. Do not put it on Phonix. She already left, and she is not this joke.

## 8. Six before the minute

The early-rush joke. Six small things, too soon, and a clock.

**Placement.** The Parcel Moors, as a rare pack: six Nippers, not a new species with a health bar, but six of the cheapest tick already in that zone, spawned in a tight line from one crate, with a toast. Or a bounty board line in Stillwater: "Six before the minute. Pay is bad. They will be here before you are dressed." Completing it is killing six machines in the first minute after entering the Moors, once per character.

**2087 twist.** "Six before the minute." The pool is a terms update that went live while you were reading the title. HOA-9 did not authorise it and will fine you for the mess. No creep. No base. A crate and a bad clock.

**Effort.** S for the bounty text alone. M if the six ticks actually arrive early. Do not build a base-building minigame.

**IP risk.** Low. "Six-pool" is not on screen. The number and the hurry are.

## 9. Stimpacks

The marine bark, pointed at the stims we already drink.

**Placement.** The belt, and Brenda. When the player drinks a stim with the thumb button or Q, the float stays "+stim". The first drink of a visit, and only that one, also toasts "Stimpacks." Brenda, when she sells one and the belt had room, can say it instead of the paid line, once. A stim that overflowed into the pack does not say it. The pack is not the drink. The belt is.

**2087 twist.** Our stims already restore life and, with the right skill, energy. The marine used his to move faster and bleed. Ours are workplace first aid that the workplace charges for. "Stimpacks." Then, if the belt hits empty on that drink: "That was the pack." Brenda is not a marine. She is selling the same vial the thumb button drinks. The word is a label she peeled off a crate in the Galleria and never looked up.

**Effort.** S. The belt-then-pack rule is already how stims move. This is only the bark.

**IP risk.** Medium, because the single word is the whole voice line. Say it in text, once per visit, in our font. No radio filter, no helmet, no squad reply. If that still feels like a clip, the fallback line is "Stims. In packs." which keeps the joke and drops the bark.

## 10. Launch notice

The red warning, with nothing nuclear behind it.

**Placement.** The moment a real threat is already on screen, as a one-line toast in our voice, not a klaxon. Candidates: Pryce names his offer in the Glass Cathedral; MANDATE makes the second offer in Still Water; the player jams the servo leg and Bull Run opens. Pick one. The Glass Cathedral is the cleanest, because Pryce's offer is the weapon and everyone in the room pretends it is a meeting.

**2087 twist.** "Launch notice." Not detected, not a siren, not a minimap ping. A calendar item. "Launch notice: the offer is in the room." The player can decline it, which is the whole act. There is no missile. Drawing a missile is the mistake.

**Effort.** S.

**IP risk.** Low if we avoid the famous four-word warning and any audio sting. High the moment we play a siren or paint a red mini-map. Text only.

## 11. Overwhelming

A cheat-code item that does almost nothing and is very proud of it.

**Placement.** A junk-adjacent charm or a free sticker in the pack, not a worn unique that wins fights. Found once, in a crate in the Simulator, or sold by Wren under the boxes as a label with no box. Name on the card: "Overwhelming." Using it, or putting it on the belt as a charm with no affix, toasts the line and adds a three-second float of the player's name in gold. Stats do not change. It scraps for one part. It is requirement-free, like the other joke items.

**2087 twist.** The old cheat made a unit a god. This one makes a person briefly impossible to invoice. "Overwhelming." Subtitle on the card: "A cheat code from a war that did not happen here. It still believes in itself." For three seconds, vendors' buy buttons look at you and decide you are not the customer. Then they are themselves again. That is the whole effect. If even that is too much system, the float alone is enough.

**Effort.** M for the pause on vendors. S for the float and the card.

**IP risk.** Medium. The two words together are the cheat. Keep them as the item's boast, not as a typed code that grants levels, vision, or damage. No unit turns into a hero. No mana becomes infinite. High if the item actually breaks the sheet.

## 12. Show me the stipend. Operation crawl.

The two typed cheats, as things a person can say to a machine that is not listening.

**Placement.** The existing talk box, or a one-line field on the options codex, not a new console with a parser full of real commands. "show me the stipend" pays 1 sat, once per character, and toasts "The stipend has seen you." "operation crawl" sets run speed down for ten seconds and toasts "You are early, which is a kind of fast." Anywhere in town. The Simulator will also hear them, because that is where cheats used to live, and it will answer the same way. They do nothing in a boss room.

**2087 twist.** Money cheat becomes one sat, which is the honest unit and an insult to the fantasy. Speed cheat becomes being early to a meeting that started without you. Neither one stacks. Neither one is a secret menu. If the player types the original phrases, the machine answers with the parody and does not grant the original effect. We do not implement the original effect under any spelling.

**Effort.** S. Two strings, one sat, one timed slow.

**IP risk.** Medium for the shapes, Low if the accepted strings are only the parody spellings. Do not accept the original cheat tokens as aliases. That is how a joke becomes a port.

## What to build first

Six, when anyone picks this up. The other six can wait without hurting the set.

1. **The Nipper** (idea 1). It is the one Micah asked for, and it explains the rest: there is no invasion, so every other line is a person quoting a war they were not in.
2. **Additional relays** (idea 2). One sentence on a failure we already show.
3. **Need more sats / not enough vapour** (idea 3). Same. It uses sats, which are already the mineral.
4. **Stimpacks** (idea 9). The belt already drinks. The bark is one toast. It should land with the stim pack change, or just after it, so "the pack only carries the extra" and "Stimpacks" are the same joke.
5. **En tariff** (idea 6). One local. No system.
6. **Show me the stipend / Operation crawl** (idea 12). One sat and a slow walk. The cheapest cheat, and the one that cannot unbalance a fight if the boss-room refusal stays.

Leave Reyn until a hire pass. Leave the Queen of Margins until a memo pass that is already touching Vale. Leave six-before-the-minute until someone wants a timed bounty. Leave the launch notice until the Glass Cathedral cinematic is open for another line. Leave Overwhelming until joke items are being added on purpose. Leave barges as a line only, and only after relays, so we do not grow a second faction by accident.

If a later pass can only draw one new picture, it is the Nipper, and it is a scribble.
