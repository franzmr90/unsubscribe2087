# StarCraft homage eggs

The six in "What to build first" are in the game: the Nipper slide, the relay line, the two spend lines, the stim bark, the Stillwater coat, and the two typed lines. The other six are still a plan. Their long versions are the last section of this note, and nothing in that section is in the game. No studio assets, no audio, and no logos.

Unsubscribe 2087 already has its invasions. They are corporate. Phonix left. MANDATE stayed. Nobody in Stillwater has met a thing from off the planet, and the hero knows that. The jokes below lean on that. A famous real-time strategy game is the reference. The lines, names, and pictures in the game would be ours.

## Rules

- Parody names only. The table below is the allow-list for anything a player can read.
- No studio assets: no logos, no unit renders, no icons traced off a screenshot, no voice clips, no music cues.
- A silhouette, if one is ever drawn, is a new scribble: low, many-legged, wrong for this mall. It is not a copy of a unit.
- The shipped sentence is the 2087 line in each idea. The famous line is named so we know what we are twisting. It is not pasted onto a button.
- One bark, once. These are eggs. They do not become a faction.

| Reference shape | Name we would use |
|---|---|
| Small rusher | Nipper |
| The rush itself | Six before the minute |
| Power building | Relay |
| Gas | Vapour |
| Crystal money | Sats, which we already have |
| Transport blob | Barge |
| Hired gun | Reyn |
| Liturgy | En tariff |
| Bladed queen | The Queen of Margins |
| Red button | Launch notice |
| Cheat sticker | Overwhelming |
| Typed cheat | Show me the stipend, Operation crawl |

Effort is S (a line or one slide), M (a person, a slide with a drawing, or a small item), or L (a new fight). IP risk is Low when the line is ours, Medium when a player will still hear the original in their head, High if we would need their art, their audio, or their exact sentence on screen. High is out.

## 1. The Nipper crosses a cinematic

Micah's idea. During a cinematic a Nipper runs through the shot. The hero is confused. As far as they know, there has not been an alien invasion. There will not be one. The creature is lost.

**Placement.** One extra slide in the Act I opening, on the cut into the Parcel Moors, before Gatewarden HOA-9. The lawns are four centimetres. Something lower than the grass crosses left to right and is gone. Gramps, if he is in the slide, does not look up.

**2087 twist.** The hero: "That was not a drone." Gramps: "Dog." The hero: "Dogs do not have that many opinions." Gramps: "Then it was somebody's visor, and it got out. Keep walking. The invasion already happened. It had a terms of service."

**Effort.** M. One slide of text is S. A scribble of the Nipper, original, makes it M. No new AI, no spawn, no loot.

**IP risk.** Low if the scribble is ours and the slide never names a faction. Medium if the drawing starts to match a unit everybody recognises. Stop at "too many legs, wrong mall."

## 2. You must subscribe additional relays

The power-building line, said when a thing the player expected to work has no socket left.

**Placement.** Two existing failures, not a new screen. Casting with an empty energy bar already says "Not enough energy." A Founder at the intern cap already refuses another summon. Add the relay line as the second sentence, once per town visit, on the first of those failures. Stillwater Commons is enough for the player to meet it. It can repeat in the Updraft, where the floor is already a machine that needs power.

**2087 twist.** "You must subscribe additional relays." Then, smaller: "The free tier covers four." Energy and headcount were the pylons all along. Nobody constructs anything. They accept the next plan.

**Effort.** S.

**IP risk.** Low. The verb is the joke. The original sentence does not appear.

## 3. Need more sats. Not enough vapour.

The two resource barks, tied to money we already have.

**Placement.** Any spend that already fails. Brenda, Nonce, Kestrel's hire, a shrine that costs sats, the lockbox fee. The first failure in a visit says "Need more sats." A second, different failure in the same visit says "Not enough vapour." Vapour is not a new currency. The line means the Stipend's green remainder, the part of the price that is not the coin. The Data Lake and the Boardwalk are the meanest places to hear the second line, because nothing there has a price tag until it does.

**2087 twist.** Minerals were the honest rock. Sats already are that, in this game. Vapour is the fee beside them: adoption, social credit, the spread. "Need more sats" is the bitcoiner bark. "Not enough vapour" is Unity, charging for the smell of the trade. Mr. Float can say the second one when a loan is short. He would.

**Effort.** S. Two toasts, a per-visit latch so they do not spam.

**IP risk.** Low. We never say the gas's name.

## 4. Reyn, late of the watch

A mercenary in the Jim Raynor shape: a decent person who used to wear a uniform, still wears the coat, and hires out because the coat's employer went bad.

**Placement.** Kestrel's row in Stillwater Commons, as a second hire beside the existing companions, or a one-off bodyguard for the Glass Cathedral approach who will not enter the building. He has already been inside once. He talks about the Regional Office the way a person talks about a commander they stopped saluting.

**2087 twist.** His name is Reyn. He was Parcel Watch until the watch started fining people for the length of their grief. He will fight HOA machines and he will not fight strikers. His hire line: "I used to be the marine. Then the marine got a visor. The visor is still employed." He costs credits, not a speech. If he dies he stays dead until Kestrel, same as the others. He does not get a portrait from anywhere else.

**Effort.** M. A hire row, a bark, no new combat role. L if he gets his own skill.

**IP risk.** Medium. A gravel-voiced ex-soldier is a wide trope. Keep him a fired watchman with a road-sign opinion, not a marshal with a ship. No cigar, no badge, no voice.

## 5. Spawn more barges

The supply line, said when the player is carrying more fight than they have pockets for.

**Placement.** The Founder, at the summon cap, in any district. The first refusal each visit. The Mezzanine is the right cruel room for it, because Vale's floor is already full of things that were sent up. A second, quieter use: the belt, when stims are at cap and the pack just took the overflow. "Spawn more barges" meaning "your pockets are the barge."

**2087 twist.** "Spawn more barges." The Founder hears it as headcount. Everyone else hears it as inventory. The barge is not a creature. It is the next subscription tier of carrying things. Interns are not larvae. Do not draw a floating sac.

**Effort.** S for the line. L if a barge is ever a unit. Do not make it a unit.

**IP risk.** Low for the line. High if we draw the sac. We will not.

## 6. En tariff

A greeting that is almost a prayer, said by one person who thinks it is ordinary.

**Placement.** One Stillwater local, not Gramps and not Brenda. A new idling line on an existing chatterer in the commons, or a single node-tender near Nonce if he is in town. They say it when the hero walks up. They do not explain it. If the hero's lean is bitcoiner they add "executor" and they are wrong about the title. If the lean is fiat they say it the same way anyway, which is the joke.

**2087 twist.** "En tariff." Not a blessing. A hope that the fee falls on someone else. The second line, once, if the player waits: "En tariff, and mind the spread." No golden armor. No third eye. The speaker is a person in a coat who read one old manual and kept the hello.

**Effort.** S.

**IP risk.** Medium if we complete the famous couplet. Low if we stop at "En tariff" and never add the name that follows it in the original. Stop there.

## 7. The Queen of Margins

The bladed-queen allusion, aimed at a woman this story already has, without turning her into someone else.

**Placement.** A codex memo on the Boardwalk or the Mezzanine, filed next to Vale's internal profile. Not a boss rename. Vale stays Vale. Phonix stays Phonix. The memo is someone else's gossip.

**2087 twist.** "They promoted her once, and the promotion grew edges. People who liked the first version still ask for it. The version that remained is better at the job and worse at being asked." The queen is a job title. The blades are the terms. Nobody sprouts wings. A player who knows the reference will wince. A player who does not will think it is about Vale, which it is.

**Effort.** S. One memo.

**IP risk.** Medium. The risk is finishing the allusion out loud: a name, a swarm, a transformation scene. The memo never does that. Do not put it on Phonix. She already left, and she is not this joke.

## 8. Six before the minute

The early-rush joke. Six small things, too soon, and a clock.

**Placement.** The Parcel Moors, as a rare pack: six Nippers, not a new species with a health bar, but six of the cheapest tick already in that zone, spawned in a tight line from one crate, with a toast. Or a bounty board line in Stillwater: "Six before the minute. Pay is bad. They will be here before you are dressed." Completing it is killing six machines in the first minute after entering the Moors, once per character.

**2087 twist.** "Six before the minute." The pool is a terms update that went live while you were reading the title. HOA-9 did not authorise it and will fine you for the mess. No creep. No base. A crate and a bad clock.

**Effort.** S for the bounty text alone. M if the six ticks actually arrive early. Do not build a base-building minigame.

**IP risk.** Low. "Six-pool" is not on screen. The number and the hurry are.

## 9. Stimpacks

The marine bark, pointed at the stims we already drink.

**Placement.** The belt, and Brenda. When the player drinks a stim with the thumb button or Q, the float stays "+stim". The first drink of a visit, and only that one, also toasts "Stimpacks." Brenda, when she sells one and the belt had room, can say it instead of the paid line, once. A stim that overflowed into the pack does not say it. The pack is not the drink. The belt is.

**2087 twist.** Our stims already restore life and, with the right skill, energy. The marine used his to move faster and bleed. Ours are workplace first aid that the workplace charges for. "Stimpacks." Then, if the belt hits empty on that drink: "That was the pack." Brenda is not a marine. She is selling the same vial the thumb button drinks. The word is a label she peeled off a crate in the Galleria and never looked up.

**Effort.** S. The belt-then-pack rule is already how stims move. This is only the bark.

**IP risk.** Medium, because the single word is the whole voice line. Say it in text, once per visit, in our font. No radio filter, no helmet, no squad reply. If that still feels like a clip, the fallback line is "Stims. In packs." which keeps the joke and drops the bark.

## 10. Launch notice

The red warning, with nothing nuclear behind it.

**Placement.** The moment a real threat is already on screen, as a one-line toast in our voice, not a klaxon. Candidates: Pryce names his offer in the Glass Cathedral; MANDATE makes the second offer in Still Water; the player jams the servo leg and Bull Run opens. Pick one. The Glass Cathedral is the cleanest, because Pryce's offer is the weapon and everyone in the room pretends it is a meeting.

**2087 twist.** "Launch notice." Not detected, not a siren, not a minimap ping. A calendar item. "Launch notice: the offer is in the room." The player can decline it, which is the whole act. There is no missile. Drawing a missile is the mistake.

**Effort.** S.

**IP risk.** Low if we avoid the famous four-word warning and any audio sting. High the moment we play a siren or paint a red mini-map. Text only.

## 11. Overwhelming

A cheat-code item that does almost nothing and is very proud of it.

**Placement.** A junk-adjacent charm or a free sticker in the pack, not a worn unique that wins fights. Found once, in a crate in the Simulator, or sold by Wren under the boxes as a label with no box. Name on the card: "Overwhelming." Using it, or putting it on the belt as a charm with no affix, toasts the line and adds a three-second float of the player's name in gold. Stats do not change. It scraps for one part. It is requirement-free, like the other joke items.

**2087 twist.** The old cheat made a unit a god. This one makes a person briefly impossible to invoice. "Overwhelming." Subtitle on the card: "A cheat code from a war that did not happen here. It still believes in itself." For three seconds, vendors' buy buttons look at you and decide you are not the customer. Then they are themselves again. That is the whole effect. If even that is too much system, the float alone is enough.

**Effort.** M for the pause on vendors. S for the float and the card.

**IP risk.** Medium. The two words together are the cheat. Keep them as the item's boast, not as a typed code that grants levels, vision, or damage. No unit turns into a hero. No mana becomes infinite. High if the item actually breaks the sheet.

## 12. Show me the stipend. Operation crawl.

The two typed cheats, as things a person can say to a machine that is not listening.

**Placement.** The existing talk box, or a one-line field on the options codex, not a new console with a parser full of real commands. "show me the stipend" pays 1 sat, once per character, and toasts "The stipend has seen you." "operation crawl" sets run speed down for ten seconds and toasts "You are early, which is a kind of fast." Anywhere in town. The Simulator will also hear them, because that is where cheats used to live, and it will answer the same way. They do nothing in a boss room.

**2087 twist.** Money cheat becomes one sat, which is the honest unit and an insult to the fantasy. Speed cheat becomes being early to a meeting that started without you. Neither one stacks. Neither one is a secret menu. If the player types the original phrases, the machine answers with the parody and does not grant the original effect. We do not implement the original effect under any spelling.

**Effort.** S. Two strings, one sat, one timed slow.

**IP risk.** Medium for the shapes, Low if the accepted strings are only the parody spellings. Do not accept the original cheat tokens as aliases. That is how a joke becomes a port.

## What to build first

These six are in the game. The other six can wait without hurting the set.

1. **The Nipper** (idea 1). It is the one Micah asked for, and it explains the rest: there is no invasion, so every other line is a person quoting a war they were not in.
2. **Additional relays** (idea 2). One sentence on a failure we already show.
3. **Need more sats / not enough vapour** (idea 3). Same. It uses sats, which are already the mineral.
4. **Stimpacks** (idea 9). The belt already drinks. The bark is one toast. It should land with the stim pack change, or just after it, so "the pack only carries the extra" and "Stimpacks" are the same joke.
5. **En tariff** (idea 6). One local. No system.
6. **Show me the stipend / Operation crawl** (idea 12). One sat and a slow walk. The cheapest cheat, and the one that cannot unbalance a fight if the boss-room refusal stays.

Leave Reyn until a hire pass. Leave the Queen of Margins until a memo pass that is already touching Vale. Leave six-before-the-minute until someone wants a timed bounty. Leave the launch notice until the Glass Cathedral cinematic is open for another line. Leave Overwhelming until joke items are being added on purpose. Leave barges as a line only, and only after relays, so we do not grow a second faction by accident. That is the advice for the one-bark versions. The long versions are the next section, and they are still unbuilt.

If a later pass can only draw one new picture, it is the Nipper, and it is a scribble.

## The long versions

Plan only. The six short notes above stay the one-bark eggs. This section is what they become if anyone builds them as side content. A player who never saw the reference should still want the person, the clock, the cart, or the choice. A player who knows the reference should hear the specific joke and never see the original sentence, the original portrait, or the original sound.

Rules for this section, on top of the rules at the top:

- Parody names only. New names a player can read are listed under each idea. They join the allow-list when that idea is built. They are not built now.
- Each idea has two layers. The outside layer is the satire, the mechanic, and the reward. The inside layer is the callback. The outside layer has to stand if the inside layer is missed.
- A running gag is one fact that gets slightly wronger every act, and then pays off. It is not a new faction.
- Fights reuse a body the game already has and give it a new name. No new species, no swarm, no second war.
- The Nipper thread is the spine. The shipped slide is one of them, called a dog. The count climbs. The late line is that there was never an invasion.

The thread, so the six have somewhere to hang:

- Act I, the opening. One thing lower than the grass. Gramps says dog. This is already in the game.
- Act I, the Moors. Six of them, too early, and HOA-9 fines the mess. Everyone still says dog.
- Act II, the Landing. A local has a tally on a napkin and will not show it. Reyn, if he is hired, says he has seen strays. He has not seen an invasion, because there is not one.
- Act III, the Hall of Minutes. A minute records an invasion and the next minute retracts it. The retraction is longer.
- Act IV, the Agreeable Mile. Residents point at them and smile. Dogs, they say, and the dogs are happy to be on the sofa.
- Act V, the Garage, before the Room. The codex gets one page: there was never an invasion. The Nippers are visors that got out. The rush was a terms update that went live while you were reading the title. The launch was a meeting. The barge was a fee. The queen was a job. The sticker was a lie that wanted to be believed. Reyn was a man who quit.

That page is the payoff for the whole set. Each idea below has its own ending as well. A player who only finishes one of them still gets a complete joke.

### Reyn, late of the watch

A fired watchman you can hire. Decent, broke, and done saluting. People who never played the other game get a companion with a line he will not cross. People who did get the shape of a man who used to wear a uniform, still wears the coat, and will not get back on the transport.

**Player-facing names.** Reyn. The Visor Still Employed. The Number.

**Where.** Kestrel’s row in Stillwater Commons, from the moment the Moors are open. He will walk Acts I through V. He will not enter the Glass Cathedral. He has already been inside once.

**What you get if you miss the reference.** A hire who fights machines and lowers his hands when the thing in front of him is a person. Tribunal Row has picketers. They are locals, not a new enemy. Reyn will not swing at them, and he will not collect the fine the machines are there to collect. He costs credits, the same as Kestrel’s other people. If he dies he stays dead until Kestrel brings him back. He talks like a man who liked the job and then read the bylaws.

**What you get if you know it.** The coat, the old commander, the refusal to board. His hire line is the one already in the short note: “I used to be the marine. Then the marine got a visor. The visor is still employed.” No ship, no cigar, no badge, no gravel filter, no portrait from anywhere else. He is a fired Parcel Watchman with a road-sign opinion.

**The beats.**

1. Stillwater. Kestrel offers him beside the others. The pitch is short. He wants the money and he wants it in credits, because he does not trust himself with a speech. If you ask what he quit, he says the watch started fining people for the length of their grief. HOA-9’s bylaws are already on the wall. He is quoting them.
2. The Moors. He walks. If a Nipper crosses, he says dog, and he does not look up either. That is the gag’s first knot: the hero has now heard it from two men who were not in on a joke.
3. The approach to the Glass Cathedral. He stops at the door. “I have been in. The man at the desk did not have to raise his voice. I am not going in to hear it twice.” You go up alone. He is at the bottom when you come down, if he lived. He does not ask what Pryce offered. He asks whether you took it.
4. The Landing, Act II. He will ride the lift. He will not ride it if the car is full of people who were told to sit. He says, once, “I used to be the one who told them.” Then he is quiet for the rest of the Updraft.
5. Tribunal Row was Act I. The Hall of Minutes is Act III. He finds the page that fired him. It is one line: grief, excessive, collected. He does not want it destroyed. He wants it read. Reading it is the turn-in. The reward is not revenge. The reward is that the page stays, and he stops pretending the number on his coat is still a rank.
6. The Agreeable Mile, Act IV. Everyone is happy and indoors. He will not sit. If you sit, he waits outside and the gag pays a smaller coin: “They look fine. That was the job, at the end. Making it look fine.”
7. The Garage, Act V. He asks to be paid in one sat, once, and he laughs at himself for it. If you pay it, he keeps the credit hire price forever after. The sat was the point. He introduces himself, from then on, as unemployed. It is the only title he will answer to.

**The person.** Reyn is the arc. Kestrel remains the employer. Gramps does not become his narrator. A picket captain on Tribunal Row, a local named Pell, is the one who tells you Reyn used to walk that row in the other direction. Pell is not a portrait and not a second hire.

**The fight.** Not Reyn. The thing that kept his job: a lieutenant on Tribunal Row, using the enforcer body the game already has, named The Visor Still Employed. It wears his old number and it does collect the fine. It is a watch drone with a coat of paint, not a man. Reyn will fight it. He will not finish it if you are not there. He does not get a speech over the body. He says, “That one can stay down.”

**The pay.** The hire itself. After the minutes are read, a requirement-free charm, The Number: a little armor, and the line “The visor is still employed. He is not.” It does not make him immortal. It makes the coat mean something in the sheet. Bad pay on purpose if you only walk him to the door of the Cathedral and leave him there: he thanks you, and nothing else.

**The gag.** Every act, someone promotes him. Watchman, then marine, then sergeant, then marshal. He refuses the new word each time, a little more tired. Act V he says unemployed, and the people who called him marshal do not correct him. That is the whole ladder. Do not give him the ship at the top of it.

**Effort.** M for the hire row, the door refusal, and the one lieutenant. L for the acts after, the charm, and Pell. The hire row can ship alone and still be the joke. The rest is the reason to keep him in the party.

**IP risk.** Medium. An ex-soldier who quit a bad commander is a wide trope, which is why it is safe, and also why it goes wrong if he grows a legend. Keep the road sign. No voice. No badge. No cigar.

**Depends on.** Kestrel’s hire row, which is already in the game. The Nipper slide, so his “dog” has somewhere to land. The HOA bylaws memo, so his reason for quitting is a document the player can have already read. Not on any of the other five long versions.

### The Queen of Margins

A promotion that grew edges. Vale stays Vale. Phonix stays Phonix. The queen is a job title other people use, and then wish they had not.

**Player-facing names.** The Queen of Margins. Draft One. The First Version. The Margin.

**Where.** A gossip chain. It starts as a memo on the Boardwalk, next to the profile of Vale the game already has. It walks through the Mezzanine without renaming the woman at the desk. It ends in the Hall of Minutes, where the job is on paper and the paper can be fought.

**What you get if you miss the reference.** Office horror. A woman was good at a job. They promoted her. People who liked the first version still ask for it, the way people ask for a product that got worse and cheaper. You can go and get that first version. It is kinder. The kindness is how it gets what it wants. The version that remained is better at the work and worse at being asked. You are not saving her. You are deciding which draft the building keeps.

**What you get if you know it.** The promotion, the edges, the crowd that wants the earlier person back. You do not get a rename, a swarm, a wing, or a transformation. The blades are clauses. The infestation is a terms update. If a later pass is tempted to put this on Phonix, it is the wrong woman. She left. This joke is about someone who stayed and was edited.

**The beats.**

1. The Boardwalk. One memo, the short version’s paragraph, filed by someone who is not Vale and not you. “They promoted her once, and the promotion grew edges. People who liked the first version still ask for it. The version that remained is better at the job and worse at being asked.” Turn-in is reading it. The codex keeps it.
2. The Mezzanine. Vale does not mention the memo. A chair near the door has a second card, smaller, “Draft One,” with no portrait. If you pick it up, a toast, once: “That version is discontinued.” Vale’s fight is unchanged. Do not add a phase where she becomes someone else.
3. The Antechamber, Act III. An usher, not the Usher already in town, a clerk named Ives, asks if you are here for the old brochure. Ives is the NPC arc: nervous, precise, sorry. Ives liked Draft One. Ives will take you to the minutes if you want the old brochure back.
4. The Hall of Minutes. The fight. The First Version is a lieutenant using a body the game already uses for a polite machine. It does not raise its voice. It offers you the easy chair, the easy fine, the easy name. Its attacks are the offers. Declining is the mechanic: a button, or a step off the carpet, and the offer misses. Standing on the carpet is how it hits. People who only wanted a fight can just hit it. People who read the memo will step off the carpet and barely get touched.
5. After. Ives asks which draft you want filed. File Draft One, and the building gets its kind voice back for one conversation. The kind voice asks for something you would have given the cruel one. That is the cut. Refuse to file either, and the minute stays blank, and Ives says “Then she remains the one who stayed.” Both endings pay. Neither ending changes Vale’s name, her desk, or her boss fight.
6. Act IV, one line only, on the Agreeable Mile. A resident is happy in the way Draft One was kind. If you filed Draft One, you recognise the tone and the gag lands. If you did not, it is just another sofa. Do not replay the fight.
7. Act V, the shared page. The queen was a job. The job was a margin. The margin is where the terms got sharp.

**The person.** Ives is the arc. Vale is the subject and must not become the punchline’s costume. Ives can be skipped. The memo alone is the short egg, and it already works.

**The fight.** The First Version, one room, one lieutenant, the carpet as the mechanic. No adds that look like a brood. If the room needs bodies, they are minutes, paper, the sprites already used for clerks. The Margin, if a second pass wants a charm-sized echo, is not a second boss. It is the carpet rule remembered by an item.

**The pay.** The memo, for everyone who reads it. After the choice, a mid charm, The Margin: small thorns, the line “The blades are the terms.” Requirement-free, like the other jokes, and not a piece that wins the Hall. Filing Draft One also leaves a codex line in Ives’s voice. Refusing leaves a different line. Both are the reward. Do not pay a unique that outscales the act.

**The gag.** Each act the sentence about her gets one adjective kinder, and the kinder word is the threat. Act II “good at the work.” Act III “kind about the work.” Act IV “delighted for you.” Act V the shared page stops being kind, which is the first honest line in the chain. Players in the loop hear a person being rewritten. Players outside it hear a brochure getting worse while the adjectives improve.

**Effort.** S for the memo, which is the short note and can ship whenever a memo pass is open. L for Ives, the carpet fight, and the choice. Do the memo whenever. Do the fight only when someone can test a lieutenant who wins by offering.

**IP risk.** Medium, and the highest in this set if the pictures slip. The risk is a name, a swarm, or a body that changes shape. The memo, Ives, and the carpet never do that. No wings. Do not put it on Phonix. Do not put a second health bar on Vale.

**Depends on.** Vale’s existing profile memo, so this sits beside it and not on top of it. The Mezzanine, so Draft One is a card in a room the player already fears. The shared Nipper page is a last line, not a blocker. This arc can be finished in Act III.

### Six before the minute

A speed job with bad pay, and the Nipper count learning how to be a number.

**Player-facing names.** Six before the minute. The Early Update. The Minute.

**Where.** The Parcel Moors, once per character, repeatable as a bad bounty after the first clear if anyone wants the clock again. The count then leaks into later acts as the gag, not as a second quest.

**What you get if you miss the reference.** You are late to a change that went live while you were reading. Six small machines come out of one crate before the minute is up. HOA-9 did not order them and will fine you for the mess anyway. The pay is insulting. The charm you get is a timer that actually helps. It is a race, and the race is funny because the town thinks you are the one who started it.

**What you get if you know it.** Six, too soon, from one box, before the clock finishes a minute. No base, no creep, no pool on the screen. The words “six before the minute” are the whole callback. The Early Update is the build order, and it is a terms-of-service patch, not a base.

**The beats.**

1. Stillwater, the bounty board, after the opening has played. The card: “Six before the minute. Pay is bad. They will be here before you are dressed.” Accepting does not start the clock. Entering the Moors does.
2. The Moors. One crate, already in the zone, shakes at the entrance. If the player is inside the first minute, six of the cheapest tick already in that zone come out in a line. They are not a new species and they do not have a special health bar. A toast, once: “Six before the minute.” If the player arrives late, the crate is empty and HOA-9’s patrol has the fine ready. Late is a valid ending. It pays nothing and the board says you were dressed.

6. Act III. The minutes record the time you entered the Moors, down to the second, and call it an invasion. The next minute retracts the word invasion and keeps the time. Surveillance is the joke: they do not know what it was, and they stored it anyway.
7. Act IV. On the Agreeable Mile the same six are on a sofa, called dogs, delighted. They do not fight unless the zone already wants a fight. If the player completed the Moors in time, one of them has the crate’s label. If the player was late, the label says the update installed itself.
8. Act V. The shared page. There was never an invasion. Optional, if a pass wants an event and not only a page: a rush you opt into at the Garage, six waves, one minute, the same bad pay, enemies the zone already owns. Completing it does not spawn a base. It stamps the codex page as witnessed.

**The person.** The bounty clerk is the board, which the game already has. The voice of the gag in town is Gramps, who refuses to upgrade “dog.” A new local is not required. The Early Update is the crate’s author and the fight, not a companion.

The point is the clock, not the loot piñata. If the player is late, there is no lieutenant. Lateness is not a fail state that locks the act. It is the other joke.

**The pay.** A few sats. A feat, Six before the minute, once. The Minute, a requirement-free charm: for the first minute after you enter a zone, a little move speed, and then nothing. The line on it: “You are early, which is a kind of dressed.” It must not stack with anything that already breaks movement. It is a toy that wins the bounty and is quiet after. Then the reveal that the number was a version, not a population. Everyone who called them dogs was closer than the minute that called them an invasion.

**Effort.** M for the board card, the crate, the six ticks, and the charm. L if Act V’s optional rush is a real event and not a codex page. Ship the Moors. The later acts are one line each until the page.

**Depends on.** The shipped Nipper slide, so six means something. The Moors and HOA-9, already in the act. The stim and relay eggs are unrelated. This is the first long version to build, because the spine starts here.

### Launch notice

The weapon is a meeting. The warning is a calendar item. It follows you for the rest of the game and it never turns red.

**Player-facing names.** Launch notice. The Hold.

**Where.** The first time is the Glass Cathedral, when Pryce names the offer. Then the Mezzanine, Still Water, the Agreeable Mile, and the Room. No new zone.

**What you get if you miss the reference.** Your boss fights send a meeting invite one step early. The invite tells you, in plain language, that an offer is about to be made and that declining it is the quest. By the end, the calendar is for a meeting in 2035, and you are somehow on time. The mechanic is a warning you can use. The satire is that the apocalypse was a hold on the schedule.

**What you get if you know it.** The red warning, with the red taken out. Not the four-word line, not a siren, not a mark on the map, not a missile. “Launch notice: the offer is in the room.” A player who wants the old sting will not get it, and the calendar is the replacement on purpose.

**The beats.**

1. Glass Cathedral, the moment Pryce’s offer becomes speakable. One toast, once: “Launch notice: the offer is in the room.” The quest is still to decline. The notice does not pause the fight and does not play a sound. If the player has already declined in a past attempt, the notice does not nag. Once per character for that room.
2. The Mezzanine. “Launch notice: she has cleared her afternoon.” Vale’s fight is the follow-up meeting. A player who remembers the first toast now has a pattern. A player who does not simply hears that the woman at the desk booked them.
3. Still Water. MANDATE’s second offer. The notice this time will not sit in the log and vanish. It stays as a line on the quest tracker until you decline: “Launch notice. The second offer. You can still say no.” That is surveillance as stationery. The building has put a hold on your name.
4. The Hold, optional fight, Hall of Minutes or the vestibule before Still Water. A lieutenant using the director body. It does not enter angry. It waits. If you answer, meaning you step to the marker and decline, it files itself and the fight is short. If you wait out its clock, it gets bored and swings, and it is a normal lieutenant. The joke for everyone: a meeting that becomes a fight only if nobody will say the decision out loud. No projectile from off the map. No countdown klaxon. A number of seconds on the tracker is enough.
5. The Agreeable Mile. “Launch notice: this meeting was held in 2035.” The update already won. The notice is late, which is the first time the gag is sad instead of rude.
6. The Room. “Launch notice: you are the meeting.” PROVIDENCE does not get a siren. The calendar item completes. The tracker line becomes the codex page, one sentence, beside the invasion page: the launch was a hold, and the hold was the offer.

**The person.** No new companion. Pryce, Vale, and the slab are the senders, and they do not grow new dialogue trees for this. The clerk who keeps the calendar is the quest tracker itself. If a face is needed, Ives from the Queen arc can share the stationery, but this idea must not depend on Ives. A single local in the Antechamber, “the keeper of the book,” can be the same coat energy as En tariff without repeating that joke: they read you the next hold and they do not explain why the book has your name.

**The fight.** The Hold, as above, and only if the long version is built. The short version is the toasts on the three offers the game already stages. Those toasts are the egg. The Hold is the side content.

**The pay.** Information, which is the useful kind. After the Cathedral notice, the codex lists the next offer in a sentence you can read in town: who is waiting, and that the win is to refuse. A requirement-free charm, Hold: the first time you enter a boss room, the notice toast fires a moment sooner, and you take a little less for the first hit of that fight. Small. Once. It is the calendar being on your side for one blow, which is more than Unity ever was. The Hold’s drop, if it is fought, is the charm. If the player only collects toasts, the charm can sit on the keeper of the book for a sat price that is slightly insulting.

**The gag.** The meeting creeps backward in time. An offer in the room. An afternoon cleared. A second offer with your name on it. A meeting that happened in 2035. Then you, on time, for a meeting that is you. The notice never changes font and never turns red. Players in the loop keep waiting for a sound that does not come. That absence is the callback.

**Effort.** S for the three toasts on the offers already in the game. M for the tracker line and the codex warning. L for the Hold. The toasts can ship the next time those cinematics are open. The Hold waits.

**IP risk.** Low while it is text. High the moment there is a siren, a red map, or a missile. Do not draw the weapon. The weapon is the offer, and the offer is already a scene.

**Depends on.** The Cathedral, the Mezzanine, and Still Water, all in the game. Not on Reyn or the Queen. It can be second, because it teaches the set’s real subject: the apocalypse here is a contract.

### Overwhelming

A sticker that thinks it is a god mode. It is a label with no box. It would like to be believed.

**Player-facing names.** Overwhelming. A Believer.

**Where.** Once, in a crate in the Simulator, or under Wren’s boxes if the player does not run waves. Then it stays in the pack across acts, prouder and no stronger. The last choice is in the Garage, before the Room, so it does not interrupt PROVIDENCE.

**What you get if you miss the reference.** A useless item with a personality. Using it floats your name in gold for three seconds. Vendors’ buy buttons decide you are not the customer for those three seconds, and then they recover. Stats do not move. A Resident in the Simulator, A Believer, has typed something into a visor and is certain he cannot be invoiced. He can. The fight is easy and he is offended. At the end you can let the sticker have one moment of being true, or you can tell it the truth. The truth is the better reward, and it is still almost nothing, which is the comedy.

**What you get if you know it.** The cheat that made a unit divine, reduced to a boast on a sticker. Not a typed code. The options field already accepted two other phrases, and this one must not join them. No levels, no sight beyond the minimap you have, no infinite energy, no damage. The gold float is the whole costume. The subtitle on the card, from the short note, stays: “A cheat code from a war that did not happen here. It still believes in itself.”

**The beats.**

1. The Simulator, or Wren. One requirement-free sticker. Name on the card: Overwhelming. Scrap value, one part. Using it from the pack, or parking it on the belt as a charm with no affix, does the three seconds. A toast, once per visit, the name and nothing else.
2. Stillwater, after you have used it. Brenda, once: “I did not see a customer.” Then she sees you. That is the vendor rule played as a person. Nonce, if you are mid-purchase, finishes the purchase. The sticker does not get to void a sale that already happened.
3. The Mezzanine. The sticker tries. Vale’s desk has no buy button. A float, gold, your name, and her line is unchanged. The gag: it brought a cheat to a woman who does not sell.
4. The Hall of Minutes. One minute records the sticker as a shareholder with one vote. The vote is not counted. You can read the minute. It is the surveillance joke: even your junk mail has been minuted.
5. The Simulator, A Believer. A champion using the Resident palette, easy, gold float over his head the whole fight because he will not stop using his own sticker. He drops a duplicate that scraps for one part and does not stack. He says, if he has a death line, “I had it on.” He did.
6. The Agreeable Mile. Everyone is briefly uninvoiceable because they already clicked. The sticker is embarrassed. One resident asks where you got yours. If you say the Simulator, they are disappointed it was not a feeling.
7. The Garage. The choice. Let it be true: for three seconds you are untouchable, once per character, and then the sticker is quiet forever. It got what it wanted and it has nothing left to say. Tell it the truth: it stays chatty, the three-second vendor blindness remains the only power, and you are paid one sat, because the truth is cheap here. The codex records which one you did. The true ending is the one that keeps the joke. The divine ending is the one that ends it.

**The person.** The sticker is the arc. Wren or the crate is the shop. A Believer is the congregation. No commander, no altar, no second item that does grant the real cheat.

**The fight.** A Believer, as above. He is a punchline, not a wall. If he is somehow dangerous, the pass went wrong. He should die to a stiff breeze and complain.

**The pay.** The sticker, the float, the three seconds of not being a customer, the one part if you scrap it. The sat, if you tell the truth. A feat for using it in front of Vale or the Board, called Overwhelming, which does not show on a scoreboard anyone respects. Requirement-free, like the other joke items. It must not be a worn unique that wins fights.

**The gag.** The sticker adds a word every act, and never gets the noun it wants. Overwhelming. Overwhelming, still. Overwhelming, as discussed. Overwhelming, pending approval. Act V, if you told the truth: Overwhelming, declined. If you let it be true: the card goes blank, which is the only time it is scary, and only for a moment.

**Effort.** S for the card and the float. M for the vendor blindness and A Believer. L only if the Act V untouchable second is built, and that second is the part most likely to be cut. The float alone is the egg. The choice is the side content.

**IP risk.** Medium. The word is the cheat, so it stays a boast on an item and not a code the options box accepts. High if the true-path ever grants levels, vision, or damage, or if the untouchable second lasts longer than the three seconds already used for a test flag. Three seconds, once, then never again, is the ceiling.

**Depends on.** The requirement-free joke items already in the game, so the card has a pattern. The options field, so this is clearly not a third typed cheat. Best after the barge quest, so two inventory jokes do not land in one pass. Not on the Nipper, except the shared page can mention the lie.

### Spawn more barges

Your pockets are full. The next tier of carrying things would like a monthly fee. The barge is the fee. It is not a creature.

**Player-facing names.** Spawn more barges. Tier Two. The Next Tier.

**Where.** A quest any class can take from Brenda, once the belt-and-pack rule is understood, which it is: the belt drinks, the pack holds the extra. The Founder hears one extra bark at the summon cap, the line the short note already wanted, and that bark is not the quest. The walk is the Galleria, then a beat in the Updraft, then the minute in Act III, then a sofa in Act IV, then the unsubscribe in the Garage.

**What you get if you miss the reference.** An escort. A shopping cart of returns has subscribed itself to a higher tier and started charging rent to be pushed. You push it through the Galleria to the dock. If you accept the upsell, the cart gets heavier and the bill arrives. If you refuse, the cart stays stupid and yours. The reward is one more pocket, which every player understands. The satire is a fee for carrying your own groceries.

**What you get if you know it.** Supply. The line is “Spawn more barges,” said when you are holding more fight than you have pockets for. The Founder hears headcount. Everyone else hears inventory. There is no sac, no float, no larva, no drawing of a bladder with eyes. Interns are not that. The cart is a cart. The upsell is the control group.

**The beats.**

1. The bark, once per visit, already specified in the short note, and only after the relay line exists so the two “you are out of room” jokes do not invent a faction between them. Belt full and the pack just took the overflow: “Spawn more barges.” Founder, the first time that visit the cap replaces an intern, same line, meaning headcount. The cap still replaces. It does not start refusing. Then the quest opens. Brenda: “Do not subscribe it. It is a cart. It has ideas.”
2. Hollow Galleria. The cart is an object, not an enemy. You push it by walking into it. Machines already in the mall want the returns. If the cart’s “tier” bar fills, because you stood in a marked upsell circle or because you clicked the cheerful sign, it becomes heavier and a bill in credits ticks. The bill is fiat. It inflates. That is the punishment and the joke. Staying off the signs keeps the cart light.
3. The dock. Tier Two, if you subscribed even once, is the fight: a lieutenant using the mech body, a pallet with too many labels, not a new silhouette. It wants to be carried. It hits like a crate that learned the bylaws. If you never subscribed, Tier Two is a label on the ground that scraps for parts, and the fight is skipped. Skipping is the better play. The quest should make that obvious before the dock, in Brenda’s voice, so it is a choice and not a trap.
4. Turn-in. Unsubscribe the cart. Brenda files it as a return. You get The Next Tier: requirement-free, one extra stim on the belt or one extra pack cell, chosen when you take it, permanent, once. The other pay is a handful of credits that will not matter by Act II, so the pocket is the only pay that counts. A player who subscribed and fought Tier Two still gets the charm. They also get the bill. The bill is not forgiven. Mr. Float will mention it if a loan is short, one line, because vapour was always the fee beside the coin.
5. The Updraft, Act II. A lift is full. One line, once: “Spawn more barges.” Cargo drops as ordinary loot if you overload the car, and the car still goes up. No new monster. The gag has become infrastructure.
6. Act III. A minute lists your headcount and your pack on the same line. The Founder is not singled out in the text the player reads. The building cannot tell summons from groceries. That is the surveillance joke: one inventory, badly kept.
7. Act IV. A sofa has a cart and is delighted to be subscribed. You are not asked to fight it. You can unsubscribe it for no reward except the resident’s confusion. “But it carries me.” Leaving it subscribed is also fine. The gag does not punish kindness.
8. Act V. The shared page. The barge was a fee. If a pass wants a picture, it is the cart, from the side, with a label. It is never the sac.

**The person.** Brenda is the quest giver. She already sells the stim and already said Stimpacks once a visit. This is her other crate. The Founder does not need a new NPC. Mr. Float’s one line about the bill is optional and only if the bill exists. Do not give the cart a voice. A talking cart is how a fee becomes a mascot, and a mascot is how a faction starts.

**The fight.** Tier Two, one lieutenant, only on the subscribed path. Reuse the mech. Labels, not a new body. The unsubscribed path is the intended ending and must feel like a win, not like skipped content. The dock still has a small wave of the mall’s ordinary machines so the walk was a walk.

**The pay.** The Next Tier, as above. One pocket. Not two. Not a mule. The credits are the bad pay, on purpose. The bill, if you subscribed, is a debit Mr. Float can see. No unique weapon.

**The gag.** Every act the line shows up once when you are overfull, and each time the barge is closer to being mistaken for a pet. Act I a cart. Act II a lift. Act III a line in a minute beside your headcount. Act IV a sofa that loves it. Act V a person, Brenda or the page, saying it is a fee. The instruction to the artist, in the page and in this note, is the same: do not draw the creature. Draw the label.

**Effort.** S for the bark alone, which is the short note. M for the cart push and the charm. L for Tier Two, the bill, and the later one-liners. The bark can wait until a pass is already touching the full belt. The cart is the side content, and it is the one reward in this set a player will equip because it is useful.

**IP risk.** Low for the line and the cart. High if anyone draws the sac, gives the cart eyes, or makes a unit called a barge that ferries other units. We will not. The Founder bark must not call interns larvae, and must not refuse a summon that the game currently replaces. The line is a toast. The cap stays the cap.

**Depends on.** The relay bark, so “out of room” is already a subscription joke and this is the second sentence of that thought, not a new army. The stim belt-then-pack rule, so “the pack is the barge” is literally how stims move. Brenda, already in the Galleria’s orbit. Not on Reyn. A hired Reyn can walk beside the cart if he is in the party, and he does not get special cart dialogue in the first pass.
