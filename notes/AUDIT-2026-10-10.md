value. Everything below was observed — tests run, screenshots taken at 390×844 — not
inferred from source. Where something is inferred, it says so.

## What is strong and should not be touched

- **The market sheet.** The quote breakdown — mid price, spread half each way, size against
  depth, taker fee, total — is the clearest thing in the game and teaches by existing.
- **The story codex.** The prose does the work the game needs it to do.
- **The ledger web**, including the `+` quick-add. Reads well, explains itself.
- **The skill trees now that rows 4 and 5 are authored.** The Homesteader's three columns
  (ground/frost, wearing a shape, the flock) are a real Druid analogue, not a reskin.

### 1 · Stop labels and speech bubbles overlapping — value 5, effort S

**This is the single most visible problem and it is cheap.** In Stillwater Commons two
residents' bubbles sit on top of each other and on the NPC name tags — "I got a raise. It
was gone by lunch." is half-buried under "A sandwich was two million this morning." In a
crowded fight, "Extra Strong · Arc Enchanted", "Boldfang the Unbilled" and "the Pack's
Flank" stack into an unreadable smear.

`bubble()` in p3.js draws at a fixed offset with **no collision avoidance at all**, and
there are only three call sites. The fix is a small occupied-rectangle list per frame: keep
the boxes already drawn, and push a new one up (or drop it) when it would intersect. Same
treatment for enemy nameplates.

It costs an afternoon and it is the difference between "indie game" and "unfinished."

### 2 · Merge or close the unmerged branches — value 5, effort M

There are roughly **130 `cursor/*` branches not merged into main**, and some are enormous —
`all-hands-look` is 149 commits ahead, `all-hands-mvp` 145, `cage-witness-maxi` 114,
`auto-loot` 120. Work that large diverges fast; every day it sits, the merge gets worse and
the chance it is simply lost goes up.

This is the biggest *project* risk and it is not a code problem. Triage into three piles:
land it, rebase it, or delete it. Anything over 100 commits behind should be decided this
week, not next month.

### 3 · Give hostility D2's friction — value 4, effort M

Today hostility has exactly one piece of friction: a five-second grace on entering a
district. D2 1.08 had a stack of deliberate anti-griefing *mechanics*, and they are why its
PvP felt designed rather than chaotic:

- declared **only from town**, though the target can be anywhere
- a **level floor** (clvl 9) so new characters cannot be farmed
- **once per minute** per opponent
- declaring **destroys your own town portals**
- a **ten-second lockout** before you can leave town after declaring
- un-hostiling is **asymmetric** — they stay hostile to you

The asymmetry is the clever one: it keeps the stakes real while every other rule removes the
ambush. Adding even three of these turns PvP from a toggle into a decision.

### 4 · Scale drop frequency with party size — value 4, effort S

Right now a bigger game means **tougher monsters and more experience and nothing else**.
Monster health scales `1+.5*(n−1)`, experience likewise, and drops do not move at all. So
grouping costs you time and pays you only levels.

D2's answer was one line: the chance something drops is `1 − 0.75^n` — 25% solo, 44% at two,
76% at five, 90% at eight. **Frequency only, never quality**, which is what stops it being
an exploit. That is a few hours' work and it makes parties pay for themselves.

### 5 · Prove readability at eight players — value 4, effort S

The V1–V5 work added per-skill shapes, lasting-state patches, and remote players' casts on
the wire, with a Full/Normal/Thin control. That is the right architecture. What has not been
shown is **what eight players casting at once actually looks like on a 390px screen.**

The harness already exists — `server/test/live.js` drives two real browsers. Push it to
four or six, have them all cast, and screenshot it. Either Thin is enough, or it is not, and
right now nobody knows which.

### 6 · Land the stranded side content — value 3, effort M

The game has exactly **one secret zone** (the Bull Run) and four side quests. Meanwhile
there are branches carrying an "All-Hands cabinet" — a platform brawler inside the rig — and
six long StarCraft-flavoured arcs, none of it merged.

The Bull Run is the model: a rumour, an absurd prerequisite, a memo that denies it exists.
More of those, each with its own in-fiction denial, is the cheapest personality the game can
buy. The content is already written; it is sitting on branches.

### 7 · Fix truncation and badge overlap in Items — value 3, effort S

Two small things in one screen: the helper line is cut off mid-word ("Glowing items make you
stronger, faded ones make you weaker. T…"), and the ±% comparison badges sit on the icon
edges rather than clear of them. Both are a stylesheet afternoon, and Items is the screen
people look at most after the world itself.

### 8 · Make the tree glyphs legible on a phone — value 3, effort S

V2 put a damage-type-coloured glyph on every skill button and tree node, which is the right
idea — colour carries the type, shape carries the skill. At 390px the glyphs in the tree are
a few pixels across and read as specks. Either scale them with the node or drop them from
the tree and keep them on the buttons, where there is room.

### 9 · A second secret class, found rather than unlocked — value 2, effort M

The Maximalist unlocks by finishing the story, which is a reward. A different shape worth
one more: a class you **find** — assemble something, or do an absurd sequence nobody would
do by accident, the way the Bull Run wants a servo leg jammed into a gate. Lower value than
the items above because eight classes is already the stated goal, but it is the kind of
thing people make videos about.

### 10 · Compare-on-hover for gear — value 2, effort M

The pack shows ±% against what you are wearing, which is most of the benefit already. A full
side-by-side on long-press would help at the margin. Listed last honestly: the cheap version
is already there.

## Two things I could not assess

**Balance in Acts III–V** is still unmeasured, as it has been since the start. No amount of
reading tells you whether a fight is tuned; it needs playing or a bot that plays. Grok's
`notes/balance-acts-3-5.md` and the playthrough notes are the closest thing and worth
reading before anyone guesses.

**Audio.** The game has `sfx` and `music` with per-zone tracks. I did not listen to it, so I
have no opinion worth the words.
