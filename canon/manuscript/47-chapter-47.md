# Chapter 47
**February – April 2015**

Ubiquitous Enterprises did not go bankrupt. Companies of that size rarely do anything so tidy. It was suspended on the Thursday, delisted on the following Wednesday, and disassembled over nine weeks by people in suits who had never been inside the building, and the advertising business — which was profitable and legal and had nothing to do with anything — was bought for two hundred and ten million dollars by a competitor and continues to operate today under a different name.

The cluster was not sold. Four thousand square metres of it went into three unmarked trucks on the second weekend in February, and there is no purchase order, and the line item in the administration's report is one hundred and six thousand dollars for "site remediation."

Beatus Benedikt Fassbinder was indicted on thirty-one counts and never tried. He surrendered on the third of February at a federal building at nine in the morning, wearing a suit, and the photograph of it ran for a week.

He gave up nobody. That is a matter of record and it surprised everyone, including the prosecutors, who had assumed on Monday that he would be trading by Friday. He was offered eleven years for Halloran and the arrangement behind him and he sat in a room with his lawyer and a legal pad and declined it, and when he was asked why — not by the government; by his own attorney, in the car — he said that he had spent his whole life being what somebody else had arranged for him to be, and that he was going to do exactly one thing on his own, and that this was it.

He served nine years of a twenty-two year sentence at a federal institution in Pennsylvania. He taught arithmetic to men who could not do arithmetic. He was good at it. Nobody ever wrote about that and there is no reason anybody would have; the only person who ever knew was a twenty-four-year-old from Baltimore who could not read a rent statement in 2019 and can now, and who has never once told the story, because it does not seem like his to tell.

Ramón Montoya was released on the eleventh of February after sixteen days. His attorney received the eleven camera angles at 9:00 a.m. on the twenty-ninth of January along with everyone else who received anything that morning, and by the time the state understood what it had, four newspapers had it too.

He sued. He won. It took six years and he was paid four point one million dollars by the county in 2021, and he has said, repeatedly, in the two interviews he has ever given, that he would like it noted that the thing which freed him was a camera that moved by itself, and that nobody has ever explained that to him, and that he thinks about it every day.

Officer Michael Kessler served four years. Officer Ray Boone did not live to be charged. His daughter was eleven.

Marc resigned on the Thursday and pleaded on the following March and the city held a special election in June that had a turnout of nineteen percent.

Daniel Ruiz was not reinstated. He found work in April at a smaller firm for eleven thousand dollars less, and his wife had the hip done in 2017, and he does not know, and will never know, that a machine looked at a man's heart rate and decided.
