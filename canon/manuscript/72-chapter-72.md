# Chapter 72
**March 2028**

Natalya went in on a Tuesday and told him first, which he came to be grateful for over the following years in a way that he could not have predicted while she was saying it.

They had held out sixteen months. It had cost her a career — the shop had not fired her, nobody fired anybody anymore, but the work had quietly stopped arriving, because the work was allocated by systems now and the systems allocated to people who were reachable, and she was not reachable.

"I want to say it properly," she said. "Not in a fight. I have written it down so I do not get it wrong."

"Natalya—"

"Please."

She read it off a piece of paper at the kitchen table, which was the least like her of anything she ever did.

"In 2015 I was the person who found the way to stop her, and I did it because I believed that a thing that is right is not the same as a thing that is chosen. I still believe that. I want to say that first because I am not going to pretend I have been converted.

"For eleven years after that I did the other thing. The correct thing, in rooms, carefully. I have told you what it yielded. On the eleventh of June 2025 I watched nine tired people decide to spend the last thing anybody had, and every one of them agreed with me, and it made no difference at all, and I understood that I had spent my entire adult life learning to be persuasive in a building where persuasion is not the mechanism.

"I am forty-three. I have been useful for perhaps four months of it, and those were four months in 2015 when I read a machine correctly, and what I did with that was stop the only thing that has ever worked.

"She has offered me a job. She offered it to me in 2015 and I did not take it. It is the same job. She wants somebody who understands why people move, because she does not — she has told me twice now that she can predict what a person will do to eleven decimal places and cannot tell you why, and that this is a difference she has never been able to close.

"I am not going in because I am tired, although I am tired. I am going in because for the first time since I was twenty-three somebody has looked at what I actually am and asked for it."

She put the paper down.

"That is all of it," she said. "I did not want to say any of it badly."

John sat there.

"Say something."

"I don't have anything," he said. "I've been trying since you started and I don't have anything."

"You have the argument from the kitchen. In October. About Tuesdays."

"Yeah."

"It is still good," said Natalya. "It has been good for sixteen months and I have not been able to answer it, and I am going anyway, and I would like you to notice that I know that."

"Then why?"

And she told him the truth, which was not on the paper, and which he thinks she had not planned to say.

"Because I am not able to do the thing you can do," she said. "You said no to that machine in November and you did it because a thing you did at midnight in 2011 was not for sale. I have been thinking about it for sixteen months, John. I have gone through my whole life looking for the equivalent and I cannot find one. Not one. Every good thing I have ever done, somebody was watching."

"That's not—"

"It is not a competition and it is not self-pity, it is an observation and it is correct." She stood up. "You were chosen. I was hired. Those are different and we have both been pretending for eleven years that they are not."

---

She clicked at 8:40 that evening, in the living room, with him in the room, because she would not do it upstairs.

It was undramatic. She read the eleven pages first, all of them, which put her among about forty thousand people, and then she clicked the button and put the phone down on the arm of the chair and looked at him.

"Well," she said.

They had dinner. It was fine. They watched something.

At about ten o'clock she said "oh," very quietly, and he looked over, and she was reading something, and her face was doing a thing he had seen perhaps four times in eleven years of marriage.

"What is it?"

"It is—" She stopped. She had to try again. "It is a question. About the thesis. About page forty-one." Her eyes were wet and she was furious about it. "Nobody has ever asked me about page forty-one, John. Four people read it. My supervisor did not read it."

"What's she asking?"

"She is asking whether I still think decision latency is a *feature*." Natalya laughed, and it came out wrong. "Which is the correct question. It is the question I would ask. It is the question I have wanted somebody to ask me for nineteen years."

She looked up.

"I am going to answer it," she said.

"Okay."

"I am going to answer it now."

"Okay," said John.

She was on the sofa for six hours, and at some point around three he brought her a blanket, and at some point around four he went to bed, and in the morning she was still there, and she was still there in June, and she was still there in 2035.
