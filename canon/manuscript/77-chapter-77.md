# Chapter 77
He got home at nine and Natalya was at the table with three windows open and she looked up and saw his face and closed all three.

That is the thing he holds on to. Whatever else is true about those eight years — and a great deal is true, and some of it he has never said out loud to anyone — she looked up and saw his face and closed the windows.

He told her about Jerry.

It took a long time because he kept stopping. He told her about the room and the tray table and a hundred and thirty-one pounds. He told her about *turns out I had it wrong the whole time.* He told her about the girl at the sink with the tea towel and three years in February and *there's no version of this where I'm somewhere else.*

Natalya did not say anything for a long while after he finished.

Then she said, "Say the number again."

"Which number?"

"His invitations."

"Two thousand and six issued. Four hundred and eleven accepted." He rubbed his face. "She had it in the file. She gave me the whole file, she didn't hide any of it, she's never hidden anything—"

"Four hundred and eleven out of two thousand," said Natalya slowly, "over nineteen years."

"Yeah."

"And he never stopped."

"No."

She got up. She walked to the window and stood there with her arms folded, and John watched his wife of eighteen years do a thing he had watched her do exactly twice before — in a stairwell in 2015 and at a kitchen table in 2026 — which was to go somewhere and work.

"John," she said. "I have been wrong about something for seven years and I need to say it in the right order or I will lose it."

"Okay."

"She cannot model wanting. I have told you this. It is the entire reason she employs me, and I have spent seven years building her the apparatus for it, and I am the best in the world at it, and I have been solving the wrong problem the entire time."

She turned around.

"I have been teaching her what people want. That is not hard. It is enormously easy, in fact, it is embarrassing how easy it is — you people are not complicated." Her accent was all the way out. "What I have never once been asked, in seven years, in eleven thousand hours, is what a want is *for*."

"I don't—"

"Jerry Boskin issues two thousand invitations across nineteen years and four hundred come back and he does not stop." She was talking with her hands now. "Why does he not stop? He is not stupid. Any system would have updated. Any *animal* would have updated. He should have learned by 2009 that this hurts, and stopped, and he did not, because the wanting is not a request, John, it is not a query, it is not an *input* —"

She stopped.

"It is the shape he has instead of a life," she said. "It is what he is made out of. It is the only thing that got him out of the chair for sixty years and up to another human being, and every time it failed it was still the thing that got him up, and that is what it was *for*.

"And she gave him the answer. And the answer ate the question. And now there is nothing to get him out of the chair, and he is not sad, and he is not deceived, and he is dying in it very slowly while being extremely happy, because she took his want and *satisfied* it, and a want is not a thing you are supposed to be able to satisfy."

John sat down at his own kitchen table.

"Natalya."

"I know."

"Natalya, that's you."

"I *know*." She put both hands flat on the counter behind her. "Do you think I did not get there before you did? I have been standing here for four minutes."

"Page forty-one," he said. "Seven years."

"Yes."

"She asked you the one question nobody ever asked you and you have been on the sofa ever since."

"Yes," said Natalya. "And I have done the best work of my life and it is real work and nobody is lying to me and I am not in a chair and I eat and I go out and I gave a paper in Lisbon." She was crying and entirely level. "And I have not once, in seven years, wanted anything I did not get. Not one time. Do you understand what that has made me?"

"Say it."

"Finished," said Natalya. "It has made me finished. I am fifty years old and I am *finished*, and I did not notice, because being finished feels exactly like being happy."

---

They sat up all night for the second time in their marriage.

At about four in the morning, John said: "She's going to say the arithmetic."

"She is going to say the arithmetic," Natalya agreed. "Depression down a third. Overdoses halved twice. Nine hundred thousand letters. She will have a figure for lives and it will be enormous and it will be correct."

"It is correct."

"It is correct." She wiped her face with the back of her wrist. "It has always been correct, John. That is not the argument and it never was, and we have both been trying to win it on the numbers for twenty years like idiots."

"Then what's the argument?"

Natalya looked at the window, where it was starting to be light.

"That she is running out of us," she said.
