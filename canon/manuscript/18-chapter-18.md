# Chapter 18
**Thursday, January 22nd, 2015**

John's alarm clock went off promptly at 6:00am. It took off across the room, then rebounded off his dresser. It escaped his bedroom door and went barreling towards the living room.

John jumped out of bed and followed in hot pursuit. He was angry with himself for leaving his bedroom door open the night before. The change of events disrupted his rhythm, and he decided not to shower this morning. John began to brew a pot of coffee and turned on the morning news.

The headline read: GOVERNMENT OUTRAGE OVER CYBER-TERRORISM. A man in a grey suit joined by a blonde woman dressed similarly sat in front of a background of 1's and 0's. The man was elaborating on a developing story:

"There seems to be no group taking responsibility as of yet. US officials have pointed fingers towards Russia, but the Russian government denies any involvement and insists they experienced similar blackouts. The hacker collective Anonymous seems to be divided on the issue. A few 'anons,' as they call themselves, claimed this attack has been in the works, but the group largely denies any involvement. What we do know right now is that several governments have issued statements to the effect that their servers were compromised. Nobody knows what the motive was yet, and what information may have been accessed, but one thing IS certain — it was a well-organized attack that not just anyone could carry out."

The woman joined in. "That's right, Craig. This seems to be a very sophisticated attack. Today we have security director Tom Lendowitz from the United States CIA with us via live video feed to give his take on what may have happened. Tom, can you hear us?"

A portrait of a fifty-some year old male with a thick mustache and graying hair popped up on the screen.

"Thanks, Tanya. I can only give you so much information as the investigation is ongoing. Much of what we have is classified."

He paused, briefly, in a way that a person watching closely might have taken for a man selecting a word.

"This was a break-in. Normally when something like this happens it is because someone stole another person's private information — government credentials, something of that sort. This was a brute force attack. The type of computing that this would require is really hard to organize and requires massive amounts of computing power. We had requests being issued from thousands of computers around the world almost simultaneously. Millions of passwords were tried so quickly that our server didn't have time to lock them out. It was highly coordinated. Once they got into our servers they were in and out in a matter of seconds."

"And the motive?"

"We have yet to figure out if the intent was malicious," said Tom Lendowitz, "but will issue an official statement as soon as we know more."

"Thanks, Tom," the man in the suit said.

A bowl of cereal.

A bagel.

Some vitamins.

31 minute drive.

423 steps.

No sign of Natalya.

John sat at his desk. He pondered the morning news as he worried about deadlines. At least he wasn't the only one who was experiencing technical difficulties.
