# Chapter 51
**2016**

Nothing happened in 2016, which is a sentence that can only be written afterward.

John wrote scheduling software for hospitals. It was not beneath him and he was surprised by how much he minded that it was not beneath him. The work was real. If the algorithm was wrong, a nurse worked nineteen days in a row, and one time in March he found a rounding error that had been doing exactly that to somebody in Tulsa since 2013, and he fixed it in an afternoon and thought about it for a week.

He was not sure what to do with the fact that this was the most good he had ever done.

Natalya's policy shop put her on procurement transparency, which she said, at a dinner, was the most boring three words in the English language and also where all the money is, and she was right about both, and by August she was running it.

They bought a house in the spring. It had a garage.

---

The tail he noticed in October, and he noticed it the way you notice a smell in your own house — not all at once, and after it had been there a long time.

A grey Malibu on the Tuesday. On the Thursday, the same one, different plate. He might have let that go. What he could not let go was that on the following Wednesday he took a route home that he had never taken, because of construction on Ninth, and there was no car behind him at all for eleven minutes, and then there was one waiting at the intersection he was going to reach in ninety seconds, facing the right way.

Nobody had followed him. Somebody had known where he would come out.

He sat in his own driveway for a while.

The thing he felt was not fear. He examined it for several days afterward because it seemed important to be accurate about it, and what he arrived at was: *finally.*

Somebody else thought it was real.

He had spent twenty months as the only person on earth who believed a thing, which is a condition that does something to a man that he cannot see happening. And here were six cars and a budget and presumably a room with a table in it, and all of it existed because somebody with clearance had read the same evidence and reached the same conclusion, and John Freeman had never in his life been so relieved to be a suspect.

He did not tell Natalya about that either.

---

The car conversation was in November, on the interstate, coming back from her office party.

She had had three glasses of something and he had had none because he was driving, and they were forty minutes from home, and it was raining, and she said, to the windshield:

"Do you think about her."

He did not pretend to misunderstand. That is worth something.

"Every day."

"Mm." She watched the wipers. "I think about the second number. Nine hundred thousand. I think about it at work, actually, which is very stupid, because at work I am looking at procurement and it is not— it is not the same thing at all." Her accent came out further when she drank; it always did. "But I am sitting in a room and there is a man who has arranged eleven million dollars to go somewhere it should not go, and everybody in the room knows, and everybody knows everybody knows, and we will write a report and the report will be correct and nothing will happen."

"Natalya—"

"And I think: she would have just told everyone." She turned her head. "In one second. All of it. Every man in that room and what he did and where the money went, in language his mother could read."

"And four hundred people would be dead by ten o'clock."

"Yes."

"And nine thousand more by April."

"Yes," she said. "I know the numbers. I know them better than you, I have read them more times." She looked back at the road. "I am not saying we were wrong."

"Then what are you saying?"

And there it was — the place where both attempts died, in the car in 2016 and after the funeral in 2019. Because the honest answer was *I am saying I do not know whether we were right, and I would like to spend a year finding out, and I am frightened of what I will decide*, and neither of them could hold a sentence that shape while the other one was watching.

"Nothing," said Natalya. "I am drunk."

"Okay."

"Put the radio on."

He put the radio on.

They were married eleven months later and it was a good marriage, and this is not a contradiction; a great many good marriages are built around a room that nobody goes into. It was not sealed and it was not forgotten. Both of them knew exactly where the door was, and both of them, for eleven years, chose the corridor.
