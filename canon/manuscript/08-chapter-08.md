# Chapter 8
**Friday, January 16, 2015**

John's alarm clock went off promptly at 6:00am. It took off across the room and into the bathroom. The bathroom door was caught halfway open on the rug, and the clock ricocheted off of the door and came to a rest up against the bathtub. After about 5 seconds of not being able to move, the wheels stopped turning; it continued to buzz and beep.

John jumped out of bed and made his way into the bathroom. He tapped the top of the car with his foot to turn it off, and repositioned it on the floor by his bed.

A shower.

Two cups of coffee.

A bowl of cereal.

A bagel.

Some vitamins.

31 minute drive.

423 steps.

No sign of Natalya.

The number was 423 today, which he noted with a small satisfaction that he would have been ashamed to have described out loud. Monday it had come up a step short, and he had spent an unreasonable portion of Monday afternoon on it — miscounted, distracted, took the last one two at a time and didn't register it. That was all. It had been 423 every week for three years and it was 423 again today, and whatever had happened on Monday had unhappened, which is how a man thinks about the only number he owns.

At his desk, John began to think about his life. He really couldn't complain about his job. He had his own office, he could lock people out when he didn't wish to be disturbed, and he could tell everyone under him what to do if he felt like it. What John did was really complicated, and he was a precious asset to the corporation.

He had job security… if someone else picked up his hundred and ten thousand lines, it would take them years of studying it to understand how he had assembled this beautiful creation.

He was forty-one.

He thought about that for a second and then stopped thinking about it, the way you take your hand off something hot. It arrived about once a month lately, usually in the afternoon, usually when there was nothing in front of him — a flat sensation of having built a very good case for a life he was not certain he was in.

Two cups of coffee. A shower. A bowl of cereal. 347 steps. A hundred and ten thousand lines nobody could read.

All of it was true and all of it was his and not one item on the list was a thing he had ever chosen on purpose.

He opened the compiler and got back to work, which is what the list is for.
