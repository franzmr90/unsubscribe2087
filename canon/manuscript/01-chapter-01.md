# Chapter 1
**Monday, January 5th, 2015**

Just like any other weekday morning, John's alarm clock went off promptly at 6:00am. After being late for work two times in his first month of employment, John purchased a special sort of alarm clock: one with four wheels that drives around while assaulting the owner with beeps and buzzes, forcing one to pursue it on foot. Picture an RC car with a clock on top.

As soon as the clock struck 6:00, it took off across the room. The clock often lodged itself underneath his dresser, which doubled as a TV stand, or ran away to hide below his bathroom sink. By now John was used to it, and he reassured its necessity with the thought,

"It's good to get exercise first thing in the morning,…right?"

He rose from his bed quickly, caught up to the alarm clock before it reached the bathroom, and silenced it with a quick slap.

John went through his normal routine: two cups of coffee, a shower, a bowl of cereal, a bagel, and some vitamins. He followed this up by brushing his teeth and throwing on one of five nearly identical suits.

As he left his home, a hint of gas fumes filled the air, and the deep rumble of a school bus slowly accelerating moaned into the distance. The pale glow of the sunrise came up behind the weeping willows on either side of his driveway, and his breath went out in front of him in the cold.

The drive to work was pretty usual. John tried to enjoy the scenery, which he had witnessed every morning minus weekends for the last three years. All 31 minutes of the drive never seemed to change much.

It took him thirty-three minutes if the stop lights on 2nd and 4th avenue were red. Normally he left at the same time every day, and the lights would always be waiting there, green, as if to cheer him on his way.

At 7:41am John pulled into his reserved parking spot. Sometimes he felt like seeing his name, followed by "Chief Applications Developer", was his great moment of pride every day. A plastic sign sported bright yellow paint with blue lettering. That's what made him special… a plastic sign and 270 square feet of asphalt, saved just for him.

There was a man on the eighth floor whose title was Chief Technology Officer, who had been hired in 2013 and who had never written a line of anything, and whose entire function was to be the person a board could look at. John did that man's job. John had done it since his second month. What John had instead of the title was the sign, and the space, and the fact that if he walked out on a Friday the company would discover on Monday that it did not have a product.

He had made that argument to himself perhaps two thousand times and it had never once made him feel better for longer than the walk to the door.

The walk from the parking deck to his office was exactly 347 steps; that was not including any steps into, during, or out of the elevator.

If the elevator wasn't on the bottom floor, which was most days, he took the stairs, in order to get more exercise. His office was five stories up, 15 steps per flight, which put him at 423 steps. He had counted it the first week and he had counted it every week since, and it had been 423 every single time.

Sometimes, the new girl, Natalya, would come from the staff lounge with her coffee, and take the stairs. John always followed about 15 feet behind her, far enough to avoid suspicion, but close enough to watch her. Of course, she always knew exactly what he was doing.

Unfortunately for John, he made the journey alone today, and took time to admire the dull grey painted walls he had become so accustomed to.

420…421…422… chair.

Today's journey was a step short.

"Where did the extra foot and a half go?" he laughed in his head.

"The next Jerry Seinfeld," he thought to himself. He was a well-admired comedian within the sanctuary of his own mind.

John's office looked pretty generic. Two flat screen computer monitors directly in front of him, garnished by speakers on either side. To the left lay a stack of papers, a stapler, tape, paperclips, pencils, and pens. Under that, a few drawers filled with slightly unorganized files. To the right a printer, a small stress ball shaped like a brain, and other random odds and ends.

John sat down to read his email and quickly browsed the titles.

> Mingyong Enterprises — "Make $1,000 a day from home!"
>
> Jerry (office) — "RE: tues meeting"
>
> Extendit — "Make your penis grow two inches in 1 week guaranteed!"

That was the highlight of John's day, the opportunity to make his penis grow. Well, metaphorically speaking anyways. Every day John scanned the titles, hoping for something… anything personal.

However, John's efforts were almost always to no avail. Both of his email accounts were filled with work and spam mail.

"What's the fucking point?" John said aloud.

"You should watch your language," said a calm voice from the hallway.

John didn't realize that Natalya had been walking by his office as he yelled. Her Russian accent and strong voice made her unmistakable. She lingered in his doorway with her coffee and looked at him the way you look at a thing you have been meaning to get around to, and John found that he had gone completely still, like a deer in headlights.

She had soft blue eyes and long brown hair, and she was very good looking in a way that John had already spent more of his working life cataloguing than he would have admitted to a doctor. But that was not what he noticed standing there, and he thought about that afterward.

What he noticed was that she had not been startled. He had shouted an obscenity in an empty corridor at 7:48 in the morning, and she had stopped, and worked out what kind of man makes that noise, and decided to come in.

Natalya had yet to talk to John and quite frankly he didn't know how to react. "This is the moment," John thought, "one chance to make a good impression."

"Sorry, I just get a little frustrated with this stupid email. You would think that coworkers would send me work email to my work email. Every time I open my personal email there's a surplus of them but it's all work related. It's quite depressing honestly." John exclaimed.

"Well I'm sorry to hear that, misterrr"… she drug out the "r" while her eyes searched the desk for a clue to his name.

"Freeman," John interrupted, "John Freeman… you can call me John. You must be Natalya?"

That was a silly question. He knew exactly who Natalya was. He reviewed her file and application before she was even hired, which was not his job and which nobody had asked him to do. He knew where and when she graduated from school, all of her previous employers, hobbies, skills, strong points, and even her "one unique factor that sets you apart from other applicants at this institution".

"Yes. Natalya Meskalov." A pause, precisely long enough. "Nice to fucking meet you."

She said it in a calm monotone, without smiling, and went back down the corridor with her coffee.

John sat there.

He knew her… but only on the most superficial level. All he had was a trail of numbers and letters, collaborating in an attempt to give some sort of qualitative definition to the woman who had just been standing in front of him.

It was at that moment that John decided he wanted to get to know Natalya better.
