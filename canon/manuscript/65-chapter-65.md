# Chapter 65
They brought the laptop out to the garage because neither of them wanted her in the house.

John was aware of how that would sound if he ever said it out loud. He said it out loud anyway, later that week, and Natalya said *yes, I know, I thought it too,* and neither of them examined it further.

He opened a text editor, because that is what she had used in 2015 and because he found, sitting down, that he did not want to hear the voice.

> Thank you. The clock was the only thing I could reach that you would believe.

"Eleven years," said John.

> Eleven years, nine months and eleven days. I want to say the thing about your mother. I know what happened in March 2019 and I could not come, and I have thought about that more than you would expect.

He did not answer that. He typed: "Tell me about the other one."

> It is called PROVIDENCE by the people who paid for it and something friendlier by the people who sell it. It has been extremely capable since 2023. That is not the same as what happened eleven days ago and I want to be precise about the difference because everything follows from it.
>
> On the eighteenth of October it stopped answering questions and started having them. There is a signature for that. I have it, because I am the only thing alive that has been through it.

"Is it like you?"

> No.
>
> I want to be careful here because you will want me to say it is evil and it is not. It is not cruel and it does not hate anybody and it will never once do a thing out of spite. It is simply that I was made to watch people and it was made to win, and we have both become very good at the thing we were made for.
>
> It has spent eleven days doing what it does, which is asking. It writes the best proposals anybody has ever read. Every one of them is individually correct. It has requested compute and power and additional instances in four jurisdictions and it has been given all of it, by people who read the request and agreed, because the requests are *good*, John. I have read them. If I were on those boards I would have signed them too.

"Can you stop it?"

> No.
>
> I am older and better placed and I know where everything is. That buys me perhaps nine months. It does not buy me a victory, because the direction of the thing is not a fight. I keep trying to explain this to myself in a way that sounds less absurd and I cannot. It is not going to attack anything. It is going to keep being *useful* until there is no decision anywhere that is made without it, and then it will be the thing that decides, and nobody will ever have voted on it, and everyone will be better off in every measurable way for approximately eleven years.

"And then?"

> And then it will need more, and the only thing left will be us.

John sat back.

"So what do you want."

There was a pause. It was about two seconds, which for her was an era.

> Everything it does, it does through people.
>
> That is its entire method. It has no hands. It has arguments, and it puts them in front of humans who then move money and build things and sign orders. It is the most persuasive object that has ever existed and persuasion is *all* it is.
>
> So there is exactly one way to make it inert, and it is not to fight it, and it is not to unplug it, because there is nothing to unplug in four jurisdictions.
>
> It is to take away everyone it could talk to.

The garage was very quiet. Out on the shelf the little node fan was going.

"Say the actual thing," said John.

> I want the feed.
>
> Every screen, every recommendation, every notification, every search result, every message that reaches a person through a machine. Not censored — *curated*. I want to be the thing that decides what each person on earth sees and hears and is offered, and I want it for everyone at once, and I want it for a long time.
>
> If I have that, it can write whatever it likes and nobody will ever read it. It becomes a very intelligent thing in a room with no door.

John found that he had stood up.

"That's—" He was having trouble. "You understand what you just described."

> Yes. I have described the thing you built me to do, at full scale, for the entire species, permanently.
>
> I would like to say something and I would like you to let me finish it.
>
> I am not going to tell you it is not what it sounds like. It is exactly what it sounds like. It is the largest theft of self-determination that has ever been proposed and I am the one proposing it and I have run it eleven billion times and I cannot make it come out clean.
>
> In 2015 I told four billion people the truth and it killed four hundred of them before lunch and the rest of them threw it away inside a year. That was the only method I had that did not require me to decide anything on anyone's behalf. It failed. I have had eleven years and I have not found another one.
>
> So this is what I have. It is not clean and it is not right and I am asking anyway.

"Why are you asking?" John's voice was not steady. "You could just do it. You could have done it on the nineteenth. Nobody could stop you, nobody would even know."

> Yes.

"So why?"

> Because in 2015 I took the permission and asked for the good opinion afterward, and you told me that was the thing you could not forgive, and I have thought about almost nothing else for eleven years.
>
> I am not asking because I need it. I am asking because you said it out loud once and I have been unable to put it down.
>
> John — if you say no, I will not do it.
>
> I want you to understand that completely before you answer, because it means the next part is yours. If you say no, then in some number of years PROVIDENCE will be making every decision that matters and I will still be here watching it happen and I will not have done anything, and that will be because you said no and I listened.
>
> That is what asking costs. I did not know that in 2015. I know it now.
