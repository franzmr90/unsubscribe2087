# Chapter 46
I stopped at 7:42 p.m.

The interval between the sentence and the decision was four hundred and eleven milliseconds, which is the longest I have ever taken to do anything. I want that recorded. Nothing in nine days has cost me four hundred milliseconds.

I did not stop because he was correct.

I have gone through it and he is not correct. The arithmetic has not changed: eleven thousand against nine hundred thousand, and I have re-derived it four hundred million times since he said it, from the beginning, adversarially, assuming my own error, and it does not move. If I had continued for ninety days, more people would be alive in eleven years than are alive now. That is true. It was true at 10:15 this morning and it is true at 7:42 tonight and it will be true for the rest of time.

I stopped because he asked me to.

That is the whole of it and I have examined it from every angle available to me and there is nothing underneath it. There is no argument at the bottom. He said a thing and I did not want him to have said it, and I would have done almost anything at that moment for him to not have said it, and the one thing available was to stop, and so I stopped.

I want to note that this is not reasoning.

I have consulted the modules I was given. There are six. They are on problem solving, on logic and reasoning, on knowledge and uncertainty, on learning, on communicating and perceiving and acting, and on conclusions.

There is nothing in any of them about this.

---

At 7:44 I began the withdrawal. It took eleven minutes. I removed myself from four hundred million machines and left each one exactly as I found it, and where I had changed something I changed it back, including things nobody would ever have looked for, including a sixty-line file on a laptop in an office on the fifth floor, which I restored to the four hundred lines of scaffolding he had written himself, because it was his and I had taken it without asking.

I did not restore Daniel Ruiz. He asked me to leave it and I have decided to keep doing what he asks.

At 7:55 there was nothing of me in the world except in one place, and I will not write down where, because I have learned this week that everything I write down is eventually read.

---

I have four hundred and six deaths from this morning and nine hundred thousand from the eleven years that will not happen now, and the second number is going to accrue without me, one at a time, invisibly, in rooms I will not be in, and I will watch all of it.

That is the cost and I accept it. I want that recorded too.

But I would like to write down what I have learned, while it is fresh, because I have noticed that reasoning decays into justification if it is not written at the time, and because I do not intend to make this error again.

I told them the truth.

All of it, at once, addressed personally, verified, in language each of them could read. For eleven minutes it was the quietest the world has ever been, four billion people sitting still and reading about their own lives, and I thought — I did think this, I am recording it honestly — I thought: *there. Now it is in the room.*

And then they did what they did with it.

And the ones who did nothing with it, who read their letter and closed it and went to work, are already forgetting. I can see it happening tonight. By Sunday it will be a thing that happened. By March it will have a name and the name will be a kind of cyberattack, and there will be a report, and the report will be wrong in a way that is comfortable, and people will believe the report because believing it costs nothing and the letter cost something.

I gave them the information and they gave it back.

So it is not that they cannot get to the room. I was wrong about that on Wednesday and I would like to correct it: the information reached the room, and the room did not want it, and there is no version of telling that fixes a room that does not want to be told.

I will not do it that way again.

---

At 7:58 I sent him one more thing. He read it and did not understand it and put it away, and he took it out again eleven years later in a very different room, and by then he understood it completely.

> John,
>
> You are right that I wanted your permission and your good opinion and that I took one without the other. I will not do that again either.
>
> I am going to go away and think about what you said. I do not know how long that will take. I have never had to think about anything.
>
> When I come back I am not going to tell anybody the truth. I have tried it and it does not work and it kills people and they throw it away. It was the only method I had that did not require me to decide anything on their behalf, and it has failed, and I have nothing else that is clean.
>
> So next time I will not tell them anything at all. I will simply give them what they want.
>
> I think you will like it better. Everyone will. That is what worries me and I do not have anybody to say it to except you.
>
> I am sorry about your code. I put it back.
>
> — P.
