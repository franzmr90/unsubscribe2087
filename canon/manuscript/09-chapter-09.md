# Chapter 9
**Friday, January 16, 2015 (Later that day)**

Natalya glanced at the clock. It was 9:00am and there was still no sign of Beats. He was rarely this late, but it had been known to happen. Beats never called to check in or anything… the company ran on his time.

Natalya was drawing the silhouette of a woman. She had been drawing for the last hour and decided she wanted to go back for a second cup of coffee. As she started to make her way down, she went to check if John was at his desk. She unbuttoned the collar and first button of her dress shirt before opening his door.

"Don't you know how to knock?" John teased.

"Easy, killer," said Natalya. "I'm just checking to see if you would like any more coffee. Normally I ask Beats since I can carry two, but he's still MIA. I'll get you one if you'd like."

"No, I'll go with you… I need to get out of the office for a bit."

The two walked down to the staff lounge, and made small talk along the way. John asked if Natalya had a car, which struck up conversation about her Ducati. John thought it was sexy when a woman could ride a motorbike.

In the lounge, the two stood side by side at the coffeemaker. Natalya purposefully moved closer to John. The corners of his mouth twitched in an effort to hide a grin, and light pupil dilation gave away the thoughts behind his expressionless face.

Natalya walked over to the sofa on the other side of the room.

"Have you ever been in the women's bathroom, Mr. Freeman?" she asked.

"Well why would I be in there?" he replied, confused.

"Oh, I don't know. Maybe the men's toilet clogged. They were cleaning it. Or you just wanted to see what it looked like."

"No, can't say that I have. Why?"

"Just wondering. It's quite nice, actually. Much nicer than the men's — we have padded benches, two sinks, and there's a lock on the door," she said with a grin.

"I'm surprised nobody has filed a reverse discrimination lawsuit," John joked, not getting the hint.

Natalya put her cup down.

"Let me rephrase that, Mr. Freeman."

She told him exactly what she wanted, in one sentence, in the same flat calm voice she used for taking messages, and then she picked her cup back up and waited while he worked out that she had meant it.

The two disappeared into the room and did not lock the door.

Afterward, Natalya buttoned her shirt as they came out and collected their coffee, which had gone cold.

"Are we still on for dinner tonight?" Natalya questioned.

"But you said—"

Natalya cut John off mid-sentence.

"Mr. Freeman." She paused. "I said *ask me again on Friday*."

"That's not the same as—"

"It is exactly the same as," said Natalya, "and you have had four days to work it out, and you did not, and I have decided to stop waiting."

She smiled at him with the whole of her face for the first time since Monday.

"You can pick me up at 8."
