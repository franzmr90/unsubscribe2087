# Chapter 57
**March 2020**

Natalya was in the room on the nineteenth, on a video call with forty-one squares on it, in a bathrobe, because it was quarter past six in the morning and nobody had slept.

Her shop had been pulled in on procurement, which nobody had expected to matter and which turned out to matter enormously, because the entire apparatus of the response was going to be *purchasing* — masks, tests, ventilators, capacity — at a speed the purchasing rules had been designed since 1949 to prevent.

She had spent four years learning where eleven million dollars goes when nobody is watching.

She was about to watch two point two trillion.

---

The argument that mattered happened on the twenty-third and lasted about four minutes, and Natalya has been over it many times.

She had asked the question. She was junior enough that it was allowed and senior enough that it was heard, and she asked it plainly, in her flat careful voice, because she had read the shape of the thing on the slide and the shape was familiar to her from a different country.

"Who is on the other side of this money?"

Carol Wendt had been at Treasury for twenty-six years and had the look of a woman who had not been surprised since 1998.

"Say more."

"You are going to create the money. Not move it — create it." Natalya kept her hands still. "So somebody receives it first and somebody receives it last, and between the first and the last, prices move. The people who get it first buy at the old prices. The people who get it last buy at the new ones. That is not a side effect, that is arithmetic, and it is the same arithmetic in every country that has ever done it."

"Yes," said Wendt.

"You agree."

"Of course I agree, it's not controversial, it's a hundred and fifty years old." Wendt took her glasses off. "Ms. Meskalov — it's Meskalov?"

"Yes."

"Ms. Meskalov, you're right, and I want to tell you what's on the other side of *not* doing it, because you're going to hear a great deal in about three years from people who weren't in this room.

"If we don't, then in roughly nine weeks somewhere between eleven and nineteen million people lose their income in a country with no mechanism to catch them. Rent doesn't get paid, so landlords default, so the banks that hold the paper take it, and by summer we're doing 2008 again on top of this, except with the hospitals full." She put the glasses back on. "Every alternative on my desk kills more people than this one. Not metaphorically. I have the numbers and they're all worse."

"I am not saying do nothing."

"There is no third thing, and I have been looking for one since the ninth." Wendt was not unkind about it. "You're describing a cost. You're describing it accurately, and I'd guess you're describing it better than anyone else on this call, and the cost is real and it lands on people who have done nothing wrong and it will be with us for twenty years.

"And I'm going to recommend it anyway, this afternoon, because the alternative is worse, and because there is nobody coming to relieve me of the choice."

Nobody said anything for a moment.

"Do you have a reply?" said Wendt. It was a genuine question.

"No," said Natalya.

"No," Wendt agreed. "Neither do I. Next slide."

---

Natalya sat in a bathrobe in a spare room in a house with a garage after the call ended and thought about a machine that had once told her husband there was a third thing, and had done it, and had killed four hundred people before lunch.

She went and made coffee.

She did not tell John about the call for six years.

---

Here is what nobody planned, and it is important that nobody planned it, because the version where somebody planned it is a comfort.

The money was created. Roughly forty percent of every dollar that had ever existed came into existence in about twenty-four months, which is a sentence that sounds like a slogan and is a measurement.

It went where money goes, which is toward assets, because that is what money does when there is more of it and the same amount of things. Anyone who already owned something owned more. Anyone who owned nothing and worked for wages found that the wages moved eleven percent and the things moved forty.

Nobody met. That is the whole point.

The central bank did the only thing a central bank has. The legislature did the thing that gets you re-elected, which was also, that spring, the thing that was right. The people who held assets did not lobby for their assets to appreciate; they simply held them, and were rewarded, and were as surprised as anyone. The health bodies deferred to the people who funded them, in the ordinary way that everyone defers to the people who fund them, without anybody having to be told.

Eleven separate institutions, each acting in its own narrow and defensible interest, produced a coordinated outcome that no one of them intended and every one of them benefited from.

And afterward, when people looked for the room where it had been decided, they could not find it, and a great many of them concluded that this was because the room was well hidden.

It was not hidden. There was no room. That is much worse and much harder to say out loud, and it is the thing Phonix had put in four billion letters on a Thursday morning in 2015 and that four billion people had deleted within a year.

*The information is not where the decision is made. It arrives too early or too late or from someone who is not trusted, and the decision happens somewhere else, in a room the information never reaches.*

She had been describing this.

Everyone had read it. Everyone had it in writing, personally addressed, in language their mother could follow.

Five years later it happened exactly as described, to all of them, at once, and almost nobody made the connection, because by then the letters were a thing that had happened during a cyberattack.

---

John found it on the twelfth of May, at about one in the morning, and he sat in the garage for a long time afterward with the light off.

He had been going through the chain the way he went through it now — the way a man goes through a photograph album of somebody he never met — and he was looking at the block that had been mined the day before the reward halved, which is a block people pay attention to for reasons that are mostly ceremonial.

Miners can put a short string into a block. It has a technical function and there is room in it and by convention you can write whatever you like. Mostly they write their own name.

Block 629,999 said:

> NYTimes 09/Apr/2020 With $2.3T Injection, Fed's Plan Far Exceeds 2008 Rescue

John read it about nine times.

It was a newspaper headline about a bailout, put permanently into the foundation of the thing, by somebody, in the week that forty percent of every dollar in existence was being created — and it was the same move, exactly, note for note, as the line that had been sitting in the very first block since the third of January 2009.

*The Times 03/Jan/2009 Chancellor on brink of second bailout for banks.*

Eleven years apart. Same gesture. Same reason.

His first thought was: *it's back.*

His second thought took about a minute and a half and was the one that mattered, and it went through him like cold water, because it was better than the first one.

It was not back. He checked, and kept checking for a week, and the answer was ordinary and public and easy to find: the block had been mined by a large commercial pool in China, staffed by human beings, and one of those human beings had chosen those words and typed them in.

Somebody had noticed.

Somebody sitting at a desk in 2020, watching the same thing happen that had happened in 2008, had reached for the only permanent surface anybody has ever built and written it down where it could not be edited, or denied, or contextualised, or dropped from the archive — and they had done it as a *quotation*, so that anyone who ever found it would know they had understood the joke.

John Freeman sat in his garage in May 2020 and cried for about four minutes, which he had not done since the funeral, and which he has never adequately explained to anybody including his wife.

He had spent five years being the only person on earth who believed a thing.

And there was somebody at a mining pool eight thousand miles away who had seen the same shape in the world and had left a note in the one place nothing can be taken out of, addressed to nobody, on the off chance.
