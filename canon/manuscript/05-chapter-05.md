# Chapter 5
**Monday, January 12, 2015**

Just like any other weekday morning, Natalya's alarm clock went off promptly at 5:00am. She had an ordinary alarm clock. A small, rectangular, black plastic box. There were big red numbers on an LCD screen. She hit the snooze button once. Ten minutes later, it beeped for thirty seconds, then doubled in volume and beeped some more. The second thirty seconds was never necessary.

Natalya rose slowly, and slid out of bed onto the floor. Ten minutes of yoga and ten minutes of meditation was the perfect precursor to a shower.

Natalya didn't have a routine. She showered for half an hour and then burned incense over breakfast. Cereal, a bagel, the morning news, and a glass of Yerba Mate… a great start to the day.

As she left home the cold went straight through her jacket, and the wind came off the pond and moved the bare branches around.

The cobblestone pathway leading from her small Victorian home slowly arched to the driveway where her motorbike sat. It was a red Ducati Monster 696, her pride and joy, and it was an idiotic thing to ride in January and she rode it anyway.

The drive to work was much more fun than when she took the bus. She drove out of the city on a back road and pushed the limits of speed and safety, before hitting a highway and jetting back to the city. Natalya parked in the pay-to-park garage across the street.

She was ready for work.

**10:00a.m.**

John was working on coding a very complex AI for linux supercomputers. He was stuck trying to figure out how to give the AI perception of time and sequence so that it could understand events happening in a logical order.

John tapped his pen randomly, to no rhythm or order. He stared into the hallway, as if the section of code might be awaiting him outside the door. John had zoned out and was daydreaming slightly when he heard footsteps and two men conversing in what sounded like Colombian or Cuban. Hell, it was some kind of Spanish.

The two men walked by his office. One was a tall, slender man wearing a silk suit.

"That suit probably costs more than I make in a month," John thought.

The other man was shorter and slightly heavyset, with a surprisingly high-pitched voice and a heavy accent. They seemed out of place in the middle of the grayscale office building.

"Probably just businessmen from overseas."

**10:05a.m.**

The two men approached Beats' secretary on the eighth floor. She knew immediately they were not from the states. She would have considered one of the men somewhat handsome, had he not worn a ridiculous silk suit.

The men approached her, and the man in the suit stepped forward. He quickly scanned the name pinned to her blouse.

"Ms. Meskalov, would you please tell Beats his lunch partners have arrived."

Natalya was surprised. The two men looked like businessmen, but only friends called him Beats.

"Right away sir. Would you like me to say a name?"

"No, he's expecting us."

**10:15a.m.**

Beats walked past Natalya, told her to take messages for any calls that came while he was gone, and left with his lunch partners. Natalya turned her attention back to the sketchbook on the desk and continued to draw.

**10:23a.m.**

John looked up just in time to see the two men from earlier walk past with Beats. He wondered why he hadn't been invited to this meeting, if it was a meeting.

John turned his attention back to the program. Something just wasn't right in the logic of it. Somewhere in a hundred and ten thousand lines there was a mistake. It could be a single bracket facing the wrong direction, hell maybe he just misspelled a command. Regardless, it was taxing his sanity.

He had tools for this. They were not much, in 2015 — a static analyzer, a thing that finished his lines for him, a suggestion engine that had read every open repository on earth. They were very good at catching what other people had already got wrong, which is what they were made of.

They were no use at all here, and John had worked out why sometime around the second week of December, and had found it more upsetting than the bug.

Everything those tools knew, they knew because somebody had already done it. They were a compression of the past. Point one at a problem that had been solved four hundred thousand times and it would hand you the answer before you finished typing the question. Point one at something genuinely new and it would confidently hand you the nearest old thing, with total assurance, in the correct syntax.

Nothing that had never existed before was going to come out of a machine trained on what already had. The past could tell you where people had been. It could not tell you where nobody had gone.

So John sat in a chair and tapped a pen to no rhythm, which was, as far as he could determine, still the only method anybody had.

John decided it was time for a walk. Sometimes a quick lap around the building freed up the tension of staring into a screen for six to eight hours a day.

Beats was gone, so it was an opportune time to fraternize. The three-story elevator ride and short walk seemed much more pleasant when he was on the way to talk to Natalya, compared to the journey en route to Beats.

As John walked down the hallway he could see Natalya drawing at the other end. She heard him coming and looked up to meet his gaze. John tried hard to maintain the delicate balance between too much and not enough eye contact. As he neared the desk she smiled, and he purposely entered her personal space ever so slightly. Her pupils dilated and her eyes widened a little; you wouldn't have known it if you weren't looking for it... but he was. John stepped back.

"So how cute was that suit on Beats' date?" John joked.

"It was the first thing I noticed," replied Natalya. "It took a lot of restraint not to ask him where I could find one for my mother."

The two laughed and exchanged small talk for a few minutes before Natalya reminded John that he had a job to do.

"I'm in the middle of a big project and I got stuck. I was just trying to clear my mind but I can't think about anything other than this silly program."

"That is most unfortunate, Mr. Freeman. Perhaps bouncing ideas off of someone else could help you."

"Indeed. I could use a little intellectual intercourse. Would you be interested in dinner? Friday around 8 we could catch a bite. I know an amazing little hole-in-the-wall place. They serve up the best French food this side of Paris."

"Are you asking me on a date, Mr. Freeman?" Natalya asked.

"That was the idea, Ms. Meskalov." John said with a grin.

Natalya put her pencil down and looked at him for slightly longer than was comfortable, which was the point.

"No," she said.

John's face did something he could not stop it doing.

"No?"

"No." She picked the pencil back up. "You reviewed my file before I was hired and you told me so on the first day, which means either you are very honest or very bad at this, and I have not decided which."

"I can explain—"

"You cannot, and please do not try, it will be worse." She turned a page in the sketchbook. "Ask me again on Friday."

"But you just said—"

"I said no," said Natalya. "Ask me again on Friday, Mr. Freeman."

And she went back to her drawing, and John Freeman walked back to the elevator and stood in it for three floors trying to work out whether he had just been turned down or given a homework assignment, and concluded, correctly, that he did not have the tools.
