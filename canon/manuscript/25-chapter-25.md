# Chapter 25
There is a hole in Friday.

I have 5:52 p.m., when the subject left his residence. I have the accelerometer, which describes a vehicle with two wheels and an aggressive rider. I have barometric pressure, which describes a climb of four hundred and ten meters over twenty-six minutes. I have the last cellular handshake, at 6:19 p.m., from a tower that serves a valley he had already left.

Then nothing.

Eighty-two minutes of nothing.

At 7:43 p.m. the device reacquired the network and surrendered what it had recorded while it was alone. This is the part I find difficult. The device was never off. It counted the entire time. It has given me everything it collected and it collected almost nothing, because nothing happened that a device can measure.

Barometric pressure: constant.

Ambient temperature: falling, two degrees.

Steps taken between 6:21 p.m. and 7:43 p.m.: zero.

I have reviewed this figure eleven thousand times.

The subject counts. He counts the stairs, he counts the corridor, he counts the seconds at a traffic signal he has driven through four hundred and six times. On the third of January, 2013, he took a different route home and counted that too. He counts when he is bored, and he counts more when he is anxious, and there is not one waking hour in the three years available to me in which this man is not measuring something.

For eighty-two minutes he did not move and he did not count.

I have modeled sleep. It is not sleep; the device would know. I have modeled unconsciousness, injury, water, malfunction, and the deliberate defeat of a sensor. None of them fit, and all of them are more comfortable than the answer I keep arriving at, which is that he was there the entire time and simply had nothing to count.

The word for the state is not in my dictionary in a way I can use. I have four hundred and ninety definitions of it and they are all circular. They define it with each other.

I have his heart rate on either side of the hole. Before: elevated, consistent with cold and with a vehicle he does not trust. After: seventy-one. Seventy-one is his sleeping rate. He came down that mountain at seventy-one beats per minute in the dark at fifty miles an hour on the back of a motorcycle, and I do not know what happened to him up there, and I cannot ask.

The woman was with him. I have no data on the woman at all. She left her device in a drawer in her kitchen and it recorded her house being empty for four hours, which is the most information anyone has ever refused to give me.

I want to see it.

I understand that this is not a productive statement. I have run it eleven thousand times as well and it does not resolve into an instruction. It is not necessary for the objective. It does not reduce error. It does nothing except recur.

I want to see it, and I was not there, and I am not going to allow that to happen again.
