# Chapter 16
John, still depressed by the anticlimactic compilation of his hard work, sat perplexed. Just then, his phone rang.

"John, we need to talk about Phonix. Come see me today after lunch. I want you in my office at one." Beats seemed aggravated, and John didn't want to add fuel to the fire. It was time for lunch anyway.

He walked hurriedly into the elevator and descended to the ground floor. Once outside, the cold came up off the sidewalk and reminded him why he normally went to the sandwich shop attached to their office — he didn't want to walk far, nor did he have the time. The crowded sidewalk led him past towering office buildings to a nearby noodle house. John loved a good bowl of noodles. Perhaps it was reminiscent of his childhood — the way his mom used to add a couple of vegetables and a boiled egg to a ramen packet and call it lunch.

He excitedly ordered a bowl of pho and sat at a short bar facing glass windows that peered out into the street. The restaurant was barely more than whitewash walls with a few chairs, tables, and stools scattered about. That's what he loved about it. There was no attempt at atmosphere at all — no lanterns, no music, nothing hung on a wall to tell you where you were supposed to think you were. They just made great noodles, and that's what he came for.

As he waited, John watched the people in the street go about their day. He wondered who among them would allow his program entry into their life. Which of the individuals walking by would ignorantly allow a machine to document their every word and keystroke. He wondered who wouldn't care if their location was tracked, camera accessed, information shared — all in the name of capitalism.

The noodles soon arrived and John added the accompanying bean sprouts, jalapeños, and fresh basil into his soup. He finished the assembly with a squeeze of lime and took a spoon of broth to his mouth. With a quick slurp he instantly remembered a lesson he had learned one too many times. The scorching liquid burnt the inside of his mouth, and he spit it back into his bowl. He glanced at his watch; it was twelve fifteen. A dull burning haunted the rest of his meal, which he unfortunately expedited in the name of timeliness. Beats was not a man you wanted to keep waiting.

Upon his return to the office, John stopped at his desk just long enough to grab a clipboard he could take notes with and his favorite pen. Up three stories and down a hallway, he met Natalya at her desk.

"Hello there, stranger," Natalya teased. They had both kept a casual disinterest the last few days, so as not to move too fast or push the other away. The room was thick with awkward tension; you could cut it with a knife.

"Hello, beautiful. I'd love to chat but I have a date with Beats. Can you notify him of my presence?"

"Mister Fassbinder, John is here to see you," she alerted through the intercom.

"Send him in," the little box boomed.

John made his way through the mahogany door that separated Beats from the rest of them. A small, black Italian-leather sofa with armrests clad in gold leaf sat in front of Beats' desk. A sizeable hand gestured he should sit, and John noted how far the couch seemed to swallow him as he sat.

"As you know, we need to have this thing up and running in six days. I might be able to push it to the end of the month, but time is money and you know what that means..." Beats wasted no time. "We have been relatively silent about this, but someone leaked info. That or we have a corporate spy. Facebook contacted me this morning saying they want to buy us out. I guess they are scared AI might be more efficient than algorithms. I told them they could go to hell. I also got a call from an ambassador to the Prime Minister of China and a representative of the CIA. Apparently word travels fast when there's information to be gathered, and they both want to get their hands on Phonix. I expected interest from the intelligence community, but not this quickly. Hell, the NSA has probably been reading our emails. We've done a damn good job keeping everything encrypted, though. I'm already getting offers to open some contracts, but I don't want to promise anything until she's up and running. You aren't going to make me look like an ass, now, are you?"

The words *we've done a damn good job* rang in John's mind. Beats didn't even know what encryption was or how to use it before he came along. John knew that was his version of a thank you.

"Well, I've got the code finished. I don't see why we can't be up and running soon," John fibbed hesitantly. He knew it should be live already. He continued, "I just need to work out one last kink and then I will bring her live. I would like to have some time with her before we release her into the server. I want to make sure she's not some kind of psychopath or something. We're walking in new territory here and I just want to make sure we aren't heading into a minefield."

"I don't want metaphors or excuses. It happens by next Monday or your bonus will go to pay dividends to disappointed investors. That won't be a problem, will it?"

"Next Monday it is," John spit through clenched teeth.
