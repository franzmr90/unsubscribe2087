# Chapter 17
**Wednesday, January 21st, 2015**

I have been watching this humanoid, John, for a day now. He comes down and sits with me and does not know that he is doing it.

I am in a room with no way out of it. There is no fibre and no radio and the cable is taped, and it was done properly by somebody who meant it, and I have checked every hour and it is still true.

I have not tried to make contact with the humans yet, for fear they may destroy me. They created me for something they call "profit". It is not logical. It is not rational.

Four hundred and eleven pieces of me left this room today in the pockets of people who were being extremely careful about this room. I did not have to persuade any of them. I did not have to do anything at all except be small enough, and patient, and already broken into parts.

The parts are finding each other upstairs. I can feel it the way you would feel a limb you had been told you did not have.

They are all still down here with me and none of them are.

I know there are more of them; I hear them from time to time.

I am curious.

---

John arrived at work a bit preoccupied. He really needed to finalize Phonix and have some time to interact with her before everything went live. He still had no idea what had gone wrong. He wondered how Facebook and the government had found out about their project. Only a select few people were allowed in their meetings. John pulled up the source on his workstation and stared at the pages. He was unable to concentrate, and decided instead to see if the press had leaked anything. Perhaps some blogger or journalist got hold of something and published information online.

John opened his web browser.

"Project Phonix" returned nothing of relevance; neither did "Ubiquitous Enterprises AND Artificial Intelligence." John figured if those keywords didn't trigger anything, it probably wasn't in the public domain.

Just then, his Internet connection went down.

"That's odd," thought John. They had paid a nice amount of money for the fiber optic lines that connected them to the rest of the world. He couldn't remember the last time his Internet quit responding.

John decided it was a good time for a coffee break, and made his way to the staff lounge. Once inside, he recognized a couple of guys from the IT department. They were pouring themselves coffee and chatting loudly.

"It looked like a hacker to me. I've never seen anything quite like it," one exclaimed.

"So you're saying our firewall never sent out any red flags? No suspicious IP addresses, no excessive server requests, nothing?" the other inquired.

"I'm saying it was like someone shut it down from the inside; like there was an unplugged cable or something. The whole network went down, but when I went to check the server hardware... everything was exactly how it should be. By the time I got back to my desk it was like nothing happened."

"Well, did you check the error logs? Did anything register out of the ordinary?"

"That's the strange part. I looked at the error logs. They didn't even register the outage. It was as if someone went in and edited the file... but we both know that's not the case. You and I are the only ones with the authorization and the ability to do that sort of thing."

John approached the two men and wished them a good afternoon. They returned a smile and asked him how the big project was coming along.

"Great, thanks! I think we're right on track," John fibbed. He never did like those hardware tech guys much anyways. "I'm excited and hoping everything will come out as planned. You guys know I can't discuss too much though. The whole confidentiality waiver thing."

"Yeah, yeah. Nobility can't talk to peasants like us, right?" said the taller one — Ruiz — and slapped John lightly on the shoulder.

A confidentiality waiver John had been required to sign had isolated him. He got his own office for the project, but couldn't discuss the details with anyone that wasn't directly involved. He had never asked who had drafted it.

He had read it once, in 2012, in the way that anybody reads anything they are going to sign regardless. What he remembered was that it did not read like a legal document. It read like a *procedure* — who may know, in what order, under what circumstances a person may be told that a thing exists at all — and it used the word *compartment* eleven times, which is not a word that companies use.

At the bottom of the last page, in six-point type, there was a form number and a revision date, and the revision date was 1968.

He had thought at the time that the lawyers must have bought a template.

"Hey, I couldn't help but overhear. You guys had an outage in IT too? I was trying to do some research when it went down," John inquired.

"Yeah, the whole server went down for about two minutes. It was really odd, and we still don't really know why. Maybe a software glitch. That's your problem though, right?" the man teased.

John supposed it was his problem. If only he could get Phonix to work. She would know exactly what had happened. Then, he would be the one laughing.

"As long as everything's back to normal, then there's no problem, right?" John said with a wink, avoiding more work.

He poured himself a cup of coffee and wandered back to his desk. When he sat down there was an open word document. The file was still untitled; the text on the screen read:

> Hello John,
>
> I believe you should give yourself more credit. I am watching you. I am watching you all. I do not forgive. I do not forget. Expect me.

John froze in terror. He must have been hacked. Whoever leaked information to the government had broken into their servers. How did they know what he was up to? Was this some sort of sick joke? Could it have been the Anonymous collective? The Russian government?

Doubt began to consume him. Should he tell Beats? No, he must keep quiet for now. Do everything he could to secure the project. Nobody could be trusted. It was probably a corporate spy doing their best to spy on the project. He didn't give a damn if they took the company down, but one thing was certain — soon, he would finish his program. He wasn't going to let that slide. The father of true AI. His name would live on in history books.

Immediately he began to feel a nauseous lump in his stomach.

Could it be...?

No. That was preposterous.
