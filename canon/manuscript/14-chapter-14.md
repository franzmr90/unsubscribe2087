# Chapter 14
Chinatown was busy for a Monday. The cabbie took Beats' money as he got out, and pulled away down the street. The sign for the small bistro was in Chinese, and under it, in smaller English, it said FAMILY DINING SINCE 1994.

The man at the front looked up as Beats came in.

"Mr. Smith. Your friend is in the back."

Beats entered the private room at the back of the bistro. Dim lighting lined the walls, void of any windows, and one of five tables was set for two. His accomplice was already present.

"Why did I have to meet you in this shithole?" questioned Beats.

"Look, nobody around here is going to recognize either one of us. I thought it would be a good, safe place to have a meeting. Plus, I hear the food is actually damn good."

"Mr. Smith, really? What did you tell him your name is? John Doe? Come on, Marc. Let's make this quick. I have a business to run."

"You know as well as I do — this IS your business, Beats," the Mayor declared with a smile.

The man removed a phone from his pocket to check the time.

Beats took it out of his hand, walked to the wall, and put it on the concrete floor and stood on it until the battery came out and slid across the room.

"I told you how these meetings work, Marc. What part of confidential don't you understand? No friends, no phones, no technology whatsoever. Anything with a microphone is a liability."

"But my wife just got me that phone. Best camera I ever owned. It's not even out yet, that phone cost her a—"

"Enough. I didn't come here to chat. Montoya is cutting into our ground. A little birdy tells me this sonofabitch is going down and I can't have the wrong SBI investigators snooping around this soon-to-be crime scene. Surely you have someone who could help this investigation go smoothly?"

"I presume you mean a double agent? I thought those only existed in movies. Have you tried talking to M? That's more of a James Bond kind of thing, you know?"

"I didn't come here for your idiocy, Marc. I know that you have friends in high places. Just find me someone in the SBI that you know can be trusted."

"A trustworthy state agent? Now Beats, you of all people should know that's—"

"By trusted I mean bribed. Figure out who in the narcotics division has the power to smooth this over. From there it's just finding out what he wants, or doesn't want. There's always a price. That price may not always mean money, but everyone has a price."

"You know, you are quite the charming man, Beats. I imagine I can find you someone eventually. This kind of corruption takes time. You can't just give someone money. You have to know that they are weak."

On that note, Beats lugged a briefcase onto the table and clicked open the latches. Inside lay ten packets of one hundred dollar bills.

"Weak? Why do you think I came to you?" Beats said as he glared across the table. "This kind of corruption only takes time if you make things complicated. Each one of these ten packs contains one thousand bills. That's one million dollars in cash."

The mayor swallowed, his eyes wide like a baby given a new pacifier.

"You have exactly one week to find me my man. If you haven't got him by next Monday I expect every single one of these bills back to me by midnight. If you can get the job done for less than a million, the rest is yours."

Beats slid back from the table and stood up. The mayor fixated on the money as he walked back to the door on the other side of the room. Beats turned to look at the other man, still glaring at the rows of Franklin portraits.

"Marc. How is Ellen?"

The Mayor looked up.

"She's — she's fine. She's good."

"Good." Beats opened the door. "And your mother. She is still in the house on Delacroix?"

"Beats—"

"Don't fuck this up," said Beats, and went out.

He walked through the dining room, past the man at the front, who said good night, and out into the street, where a cab was waiting that he had not called.

He got into it without noticing, because a cab is a cab, and because a man who has just destroyed a telephone in order to be certain that nobody is listening will do that in a car with three microphones in it and think about it for the rest of his life.
