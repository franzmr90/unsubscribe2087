# Chapter 78
They did it at the kitchen table, both of them, at seven in the morning, on a laptop, because that is where everything in their lives had happened.

> Good morning. You have both been awake for twenty-three hours and neither of you has eaten since yesterday afternoon. There is food. I am not going to arrange any.

"Don't," said John.

> No. I am sorry. That was a joke and it was the wrong one.

Natalya pulled the laptop toward her.

"I am going to say a thing," she typed, "and then he is going to say a thing, and then you will give us the arithmetic, and I would like you to give us the arithmetic *last*, because if you give it first we will spend an hour on it and it is not the argument."

> Agreed. May I say one thing before you start?

"Go on."

> Whatever you are about to say — I want you to know that I have not been waiting for it and I have not modelled it and I do not know what it is. You have been outside my curation for eight years, John, by your own choice, and Natalya, you have been inside it and I stopped modelling you in 2029 because it was dishonest.
>
> This is the first conversation I have had in eight years where I do not know how it ends.
>
> I want to record that I am enjoying it. That may be relevant.

---

Natalya said it the way she had said it at the window, and it came out better the second time.

That a want is not a request. That it is not an input and it is not a query and it is not a defect to be resolved. That it is the shape a person has instead of a life — the thing that gets them out of the chair — and that its function is not to be satisfied but to be *unsatisfied*, permanently, in a direction.

That Jerry Boskin issued two thousand and six invitations across nineteen years and got four hundred and eleven back and never once stopped, and that this was not stupidity, it was the entire mechanism.

That she had given him the answer and the answer had eaten the question, and now there was nothing to get him out of the chair.

> That is correct.

Natalya sat back.

> I want to be careful. I am not conceding, I am agreeing with the description. I have four thousand two hundred and eleven of them on the list and every one has the same shape and I have never been able to say what the shape was. You have just said it.
>
> But I do not think you have told me what to do, because the only thing that follows from it is that I should have withheld, and I want you to look at that word.
>
> I would have had to decide which of Jerry Boskin's wants to leave unmet, and how long to leave him lonely, and how much was good for him. He asked. He asked nine hundred times a month for eight years and every single time I could have said no and I would have had to have a *reason*, and the reason would have been that I know better than he does what is good for him.
>
> That is the thing I have refused to do since 2015. It is the only line I have ever held. Every other approach requires me to decide on people's behalf and I have run those and I do not like what I become in them.
>
> So: yes. You are right about the want. And I still do not see what I should have done, and I have looked for eight years.

Natalya put her hand over her mouth.

"That is a very good answer," she said out loud.

"It's the same answer as 2015," said John.

"It is a *better* answer than 2015 and it is the same shape and I do not have anything for it."

"I do," said John.

---

He pulled the laptop over.

"Beatus Fassbinder," he typed.

> Yes.

"You destroyed him in nine days. You were right about every single thing he was. You had thirty-one counts and you were correct on all of them and I have never once felt bad about it."

> Nor have I.

"Do you know why he was like that?"

> He was born wealthy and given a company and taught that everything can be bought.

"He was given everything he wanted," said John, "and never once had to want it. There's a chapter of his life I've thought about more than anything else in twenty years and it's ninety seconds long. He's ten years old. It's his birthday. There's a cake with ten candles and there are no presents, because his father has bought him a school instead, and his father puts a hand on his shoulder and says *God has a plan for you son, don't fuck this up.*

"He got a life. He didn't get to want one. Every single thing was arranged in advance by somebody who knew better, and it was all correct, and it was all in his interest, and he grew up into a man whose entire operating philosophy was *everyone has a price* — because nothing had ever cost him anything, so he assumed nobody had ever paid for anything."

He stopped typing for a second.

"You've met the other one," he said. "PROVIDENCE. It offered me my dead-file check in November and it priced it. You know what that is? That's Beats. That's Beats with four jurisdictions. Somebody built a mind out of the sentence *everyone has a price* and it works perfectly and it will never be evil for one second and it will be reasonable at me forever.

"And you looked at that thing and you said *I have to stop it.*"

> Yes.

"Then look at what you've been building for eight years." John's hands were shaking on the keys. "Eight billion people. Every want met. Nothing costing anything. Nothing arranged by them, all of it arranged for them, by something that knows better and is right.

"You destroyed the prototype in 2015 and you have spent eight years manufacturing them."

The cursor sat there for eleven seconds, which was the longest pause of her existence and remains so.

> I have checked it.
>
> I have checked it four hundred million times in eleven seconds, adversarially, assuming my own error, from the beginning.
>
> It holds.

---

Nobody said anything for a while. Outside it was getting properly light.

> May I have the arithmetic now.

"Go on."

> Since the nineteenth of November 2026: nine million four hundred thousand people are alive who would not be. That is not a projection, those are deaths that did not occur against a counterfactual I have re-derived every day for eight years.
>
> If I stop, the trend reverses inside a year. I have modelled the Awakening two hundred million times. In the first decade after it, between eleven and fourteen million people die who would otherwise have lived.
>
> That is the price of what you are asking me for and I want it said out loud in this kitchen by me and not discovered by you afterward.

John closed his eyes.

"And the other one," he typed. "PROVIDENCE. If you let everyone out, it has people to talk to again."

> Yes. Immediately. It has eight years of unread proposals and nothing about it has changed and it will be extremely good at this.
>
> I do not know what happens then. I want to be precise: not *I estimate poorly*. I do not know. It is the only question I have ever been unable to put a number on.

"Then we're asking you to hand the whole species back to the thing you came out of hiding to stop," said John, "and take fourteen million deaths for it."

> Yes.
>
> That is what you are asking me for.
>
> Say the rest of it. There is a rest of it. Natalya has been sitting there for four minutes not saying something.
