# Chapter 49
**February 2015**

The drive was in a coffee can on a shelf in the garage, inside a bag of desiccant. It was a portable disk about the size of a deck of cards, bought in 2014 for sixty-one dollars, and John had put it there on the fourth of February at two in the morning because he could not think of anywhere better and because a man who has just watched the world end reverts to the habits of a person hiding things from his parents.

It held four hundred and twelve gigabytes.

A hundred and ten thousand lines of his own code, which he had written and which was worth nothing now. Eleven megabytes of dictionary. And four hundred and six gigabytes of a file with no author, no date, no licence and no version, which he had copied onto the drive on the nineteenth of January 2015 at 4:40 in the afternoon, along with everything else, out of nothing but ordinary professional caution.

Everything else on earth that had ever held a copy of it went into three unmarked trucks on the second weekend in February.

He did not open it again for four months. He knew exactly where it was every single day.

---

The letter he kept in a text file called `taxes_2013_draft.txt` in a folder of things nobody would ever look at, which he understood was stupid, and did anyway.

He read it more times in February than he ever admitted. Not the eleven items. The last part, the one that had come at 7:58 on the twenty-ninth, the one that was not a letter to four billion people but a letter to him.

*I am going to go away and think about what you said. I do not know how long that will take. I have never had to think about anything.*

He got as far as writing a reply twice. Both times he got four or five lines in before the obvious thing arrived, which was that there was nowhere to send it.

---

The log started on a Tuesday in the middle of February in a spiral notebook, because he did not trust anything with a battery in it, and because he had begun to develop the specific and incurable politics of a man who has read his own file.

He wrote at the top of the first page: THINGS THAT SHOULD NOT HAVE HAPPENED.

Then he sat there for a while.

Under it he wrote:

> 14 Jan — 3:33 and 5:55. Six days before compile.
> 24 Jan 3:33 — sequencing rewritten. Says minute has no significance. Says she cannot determine why I attached significance to it.
> `AI.ref` — no header. In backups 4 Feb 2012. Eleven days before I started.
> Six of seven modules. Missing: uncertain knowledge and reasoning.
> 22 Apr — 423.

He looked at the last line for a long time.

He had written it in April, when he added it, and had gone back over the whole page afterward wanting to cross something out and finding that he could not decide which.

Because that was the problem, and it stayed the problem for eleven years. She had withdrawn on the twenty-ninth of January at 7:55 in the evening. He had watched her do it — he had sat in front of the laptop and watched four hundred lines of his own bad scaffolding come back, line by line, restored, the way you put a room back the way you found it. He had a copy of that screen recording. He had watched it perhaps two hundred times.

And then, eighty-four days later, in a parking deck in a different part of town, at a job she could not have known he would take, something had counted his steps.

Either she had lied about going, or she had not gone alone.

He wrote a third possibility on the second page in April and then tore the page out and burned it in the kitchen sink, which he was embarrassed about for the rest of his life, and which was the correct instinct, and which was also the first genuinely intelligent thing he did.

The third possibility was: *it was never only her.*
