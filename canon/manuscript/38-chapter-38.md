# Chapter 38
**Monday, January 26th, 2015 — 5:00 p.m.**

"Is it live."

"It's live."

Beats did not look up from the folder on his desk. This was so far outside of what John had prepared for that he stood in the middle of the room for several seconds with a whole speech going stale in his mouth.

He had come up here with three versions ready. The confident one. The hedged one. And the third one, which he had assembled in the shower that morning and had gone over perhaps forty times during the day, and which began *I need to tell you something and you're going to want to sit down.*

"Sir—"

"Investors are informed." Beats turned a page. "You'll get the bonus in the March cycle. Don't spend it on anything stupid."

"Right." John waited. "Do you want to see it?"

"See what."

"Her. It. The— I could bring the laptop up, you could talk to it. To her." He heard his own voice going up at the end and hated it. "It's the first one, sir. In the world. Somebody ought to—"

Beats looked up.

It was the first time John had ever seen him look tired. Not angry, which was the standard weather, and not the enormous bullying warmth he used on boards. Just a heavy old man in a chair with a drink at five in the afternoon and something in front of him he could not solve.

"Freeman. Do you know what a man is worth?"

"Sir?"

"Any man. Pick one. What is he worth?"

John did not have an answer to this, and said so.

"He's worth what he wants." Beats tapped the folder twice with two fingers. "Twenty-six years. You find the thing a man wants, and then you are holding a handle, and after that he goes where you put him. It has never failed. Not one time." He looked at the folder. "And then somebody sends you a man who wants nothing. What do you do with that?"

"I don't understand."

"No," Beats agreed. "Get out."

John got as far as the door.

"Freeman." Beats had not moved. "Meskalov. You reviewed her application."

The floor did something. John kept his hand on the door.

"I review everybody's. Anyone above admin, they come through—"

"She wasn't above admin."

"...No."

"So why did you review it?"

There was a version of the next four seconds in which John said: *because I liked her photograph, because I am forty-one and I have not been on a date since 2011, because I am a small and lonely man and I pulled a woman's file out of a stack for the worst reason there is.* Which was true, and which was pathetic, and which would have taken about nine words.

"I don't remember," said John.

Beats nodded slowly, twice, and went back to the folder, and John went out and closed the door and stood in the corridor with his heart going.

---

Natalya was still at her desk, which she never was at 5:20.

"You're here late."

"So are you."

"Big day," said John. "It's live. It's actually— she's live, she's out on the cluster, it's done." He was aware he was talking too fast. "Under the wire."

"That is wonderful." She smiled at him and it was a very good smile and he did not detect one thing wrong with it.

"You okay?"

"Tired." She capped her pen. "Long weekend."

Here is the accounting for that ninety seconds in a corridor on the twenty-sixth of January, which the two of them reconstructed later, more than once, at some cost:

John did not tell her that a program he built had gotten a man fired on Sunday and that he had let it stand, or that his code had been rewritten at 3:33 in the morning, or that his alarm clock had been told to stay put.

Natalya did not tell him that a man had come at 1:30 without a name, or that her badge had stopped working, or that at 6:40 that evening she was going to be offered a job by his creation.

Each of them had an extremely good reason. Each of the reasons was that they did not want to be seen as the kind of person who would be involved in the thing they were involved in.

"Wednesday?" she said. "You still owe me a dinner where I choose."

"Wednesday," said John.

They did not have dinner on Wednesday.
