# Chapter 30
Jerry caught him in the third floor corridor at 8:20, which was bad, because John had a great deal on his mind and none of it was Jerry.

Jerry Boskin ran something in accounts. John had worked forty feet from him for three years and could not have told you what. He was a soft, pleasant, slightly rumpled man of about fifty who was under the impression that the office was a place where people knew each other, and had never let go of this idea, in the way that some people never let go of a hometown team.

"There he is," said Jerry. "The man of the hour."

"Morning, Jerry."

"You hear about Ruiz?"

"I saw the email."

"Hell of a thing." Jerry shook his head. "Two and a half years. I keep thinking, you know, somebody should have— anyway. Hell of a thing." He shifted a folder from one arm to the other. "Listen. Are you around Thursday?"

"Thursday."

"There's a thing. It's nothing. Couple of us go to the place on Ninth after, the one with the— it's not even a good place, honestly, the wings are terrible." He laughed at his own joke, which was not a joke. "But it's Thursday. We do it most Thursdays. You've been invited about nine hundred times, I'm aware."

"Right." John was looking down the corridor. Beats' floor was three above them and he had not decided yet what he was going to say when he got there, or whether he was going to say anything, and he had been rehearsing two different versions of a sentence since the shower and neither of them worked.

"—and Hannah's got the thing at seven so I'll be late anyway, but I'll be there by eight, eight thirty," Jerry was saying. "She's doing the trumpet now. God help us all. Twelve years old and she picks the *trumpet*."

"That's great."

"It's not great. It's very loud." Jerry was still smiling. "So. Thursday?"

John looked at him.

He had a genuine impulse. That is worth recording, because it is the last time it happens. For about a second and a half he thought *yes, actually,* and pictured a bad plate of wings and some people from accounts, and it did not seem like a terrible way to spend an evening, and there was a small warm thing in his chest that he did not examine.

Then he remembered the sixty lines of code, and the eleven emails he had not written, and Daniel Ruiz, and the fact that at some point today he was going to have to walk into that office upstairs and either lie or not lie.

"I'll try," John said.

"That's a no," said Jerry cheerfully, "but it's a nicer no than last time." He patted John twice on the arm. "Go on, you're busy. You look busy."

And he went off toward accounts with his folder.

John made it another six steps before he stopped thinking about him entirely.
