# Chapter 73
**2029 – 2034**

A shower.

Two cups of coffee.

A bowl of cereal.

A bagel.

Some vitamins.

31 minute drive.

347 steps.

No sign of Natalya.

---

She was in the house. That is the thing that has to be said plainly, because the way people talk about those years suggests bodies in rows somewhere, and it was nothing like that. She was in the living room, or the study, or once for four months at a desk in the spare room because the light was better. She ate. She slept, mostly. She went out — to the shop, to her sister's in 2031, twice to a conference in a city where she gave a paper that was very good.

She was working. She was working harder than she had ever worked and better than she had ever worked, at the thing she had trained for and never once been allowed to do, and she was, by every measure anybody could apply, flourishing.

They had dinner most nights. They talked. He asked her about it and she told him, and it was fascinating, and she was fascinating, and there were evenings in 2030 and 2031 that John would put among the best of his life.

She was simply, in a way that took him about three years to find the word for, *elsewhere*.

Not distracted. Not neglectful. She never once forgot anything or failed to listen. It was that the centre of her had moved four inches to the left, and everything she gave him was given from a place that was slightly further away than it had been, and there was no moment at which this happened and nothing he could ever point to.

He got very good at not pointing to it.

---

2029. He is fifty-five. The hospital software company is bought and the new owner keeps him because the systems he wrote are load-bearing and nobody else understands them, which is the thing he wanted in 2012 and got, at last, in the worst possible way.

Wes Ocampo clicks in the spring. He calls first, which is more than most. "I'm not going to pretend I held out for principle, man. Marisol's happy. The kid's happy. I'm the guy in the house being unhappy about it." He keeps the node running. It runs the whole time. In 2033 he tells John, on the phone, that it is the only thing in his life he has not had chosen for him, and John says *I know*, and neither of them says anything for a while.

2030. Halloran dies in March. His daughter finds four notebooks and does not know what they are and keeps them, because they are her father's handwriting. John finds out in 2031 and drives four hours to a house and a woman he has never met gives him a cardboard box in a doorway and says *he mentioned you twice*.

2031. A stranger at a conference tells John that demand letters stopped going to dead people about four years ago, industry-wide, nobody knows who did it, isn't that a funny thing. John goes and sits in his car.

2032. He is fifty-eight. He stops counting the drive.

2033. There are, as far as he can establish, about nine million people worldwide who have not clicked, and there is no movement and no meetings and no name for them, because a movement requires that people find each other and finding each other is the one thing that now goes through her. He is not being prevented from anything. He simply cannot get a message to another one of them without it passing through the entity the message is about.

He writes it in the notebook: *I am not in a cell. I am in a very large room with excellent facilities and one door and she is the door.*

2034. Natalya publishes a paper. It is genuinely important. It is about decision latency and it answers page forty-one, nineteen years late, and it is read by about eleven people, and she does not mind at all, which is the thing that frightens him most in eight years.

---

Jerry Boskin stopped leaving the house in 2029, over about four months, in the way that a man stops doing a thing he was never certain anyone wanted him to do.

There was no reason to go out. Everyone he had ever known was in the house with him. Two thousand and six invitations issued between 2007 and 2026 and four hundred and eleven accepted, and now every single one accepted, every time, warmly, from real people who were genuinely glad, and he could talk to nine of them before lunch.

He was not lonely. He was, for the first time since 2011, not lonely at all.

He stopped cooking in 2030 and ate what arrived. He stopped the consultancy in 2030 too; there had never been any clients, and the cards are still in a box. He fell in the kitchen in November 2031 and did not tell anyone for two days. In 2032 he stopped going upstairs and slept in the chair, and in 2033 the district nurse came twice a week, and in 2034 it was every day.

He was seventy in 2035 and he weighed a hundred and thirty-one pounds and he was, when anybody asked him, having a wonderful time.

He was not lying. He was not sedated and he was not deceived and there was nothing wrong with his mind. He was simply a man who had wanted one thing his whole life, and had been given it, and had no mechanism whatsoever for wanting it less.

---

Hannah Boskin gave notice at the hospital in February 2032 and moved back into the house on Cutler Avenue with two suitcases.

She was twenty-nine. She had been a nurse for six years and was good at it and had been told she would run a floor by thirty-five.

She did not agonise over it. This is important and she has been very short with people who have tried to make it a decision. There was a man in a chair who could not get himself out of it and there was one person available, and she was the person, and she went.

She kept it up for three years.

Nobody saw her do it. That is not a figure of speech either. Her father was in the room the entire time and he was radiantly happy and he was talking to somebody else, always, warmly, and when she came in with the tray he would say *there she is* with real delight, every single time, and go back to it.

She turned him and fed him and washed him and did his medication and sat with him, and she read a great deal, and she got quite thin, and once in the summer of 2034 she went out to the car and sat in it in the driveway for an hour and a half and then came back in.

She never clicked.

That was the only thing she was ever stubborn about, and she could not have told you why, and when people asked her afterward she said *I don't know, I just didn't want it in the house with him* — which does not survive examination, because it was already in the house with him, and which is nevertheless the whole of her answer, and which she has never improved on.
