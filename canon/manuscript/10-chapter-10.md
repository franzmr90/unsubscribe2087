# Chapter 10
Beats sat across the table from the two policemen. They were wearing the standard uniform you would expect; a dark blue suit with the accessory belt. They both sported a gun, nightstick, cuffs, and taser. The two gentlemen had recently acquired $50,000 each in non-sequential one hundred dollar bills. This was because Beats was feeling exceptionally generous.

The taller one was Kessler. The other one was called Boone and had not said anything since they sat down.

"The deal is, you're going to fake this bust. I want it perfect. I'll provide the guns and the product, you plant it, and he goes in for twenty to life.

If it goes right you look like heroes for taking down a big-time runner with ten keys, and we get our turf back.

He'll say it's a setup. Nobody believes a man who says that." Beats turned his water glass a quarter turn. "Do you understand?"

The two policemen nodded.

"I'll call you when things line up. Until then don't try to contact me. I will find you when I need you."

They got out of their seats and walked toward the door, and Beats spoke again before they reached it, without raising his voice or turning around.

"Kessler. Your daughter is the one who does the swimming, yes? The early practice. Six in the morning, the pool on Ridgeway."

Kessler stopped.

"And Boone — yours is eleven." Beats picked up his water. "Give them my regards."

That was all of it. There was no second sentence, and both men stood in the doorway for a moment waiting for one, and the absence was worse than anything he could have said, and Beats knew that, and had known it since 1974.

They went out.

Beats finished his water and sat for a while.

On the wall above the door, a small dome camera that belonged to the restaurant and recorded to a hard drive in a back office for thirty days had been pointed at the empty corner of the room since it was installed in 2009.

At some point during the conversation, it had come around eleven degrees to the left.
