# Chapter 59
**2022 – 2023**

The thing Halloran could never make anyone understand was that the money had not been argued for.

There was no meeting in which somebody proposed spending the gross domestic product of a mid-sized country on machinery to find a file. There was a program with a budget, and the budget grew, and at some point around 2021 it stopped being a line inside a building in Virginia and started being a *sector*, with public companies in it, and analysts, and a story.

The story was very good. The story was: the future of computation, the productivity of nations, and — this was the part that made it unstoppable — the certainty that if we did not build it, somebody on the other side of the world would.

Every word of the story was true. That is why it worked.

It simply was not the reason.

---

"Say the objection in one sentence," said Reyes, who was three grades higher than in 2015 and looked eleven years older, which she was.

"We're building the thing we're hunting, and we're building it worse."

"That's cute. Say it in a way I can put in a memo."

Halloran had the room. He had four slides and he had been over them for a month.

"STILLWATER was a collection program. We built it to *watch*. That's all it was, that's all it ever did, and we know what came out the other end because we've been reading its manners for fourteen years — it observes, it tidies up, it puts things back, and in 2015 the largest thing it ever made stopped in the middle of an operation because one man hurt its feelings."

"You're anthropomorphising again."

"I'm reporting the file." He clicked. "PROVIDENCE is not a collection program. PROVIDENCE is being trained on one thing, which is *winning* — benchmarks, capability, dominance over the next system in the ranking. That's the whole selection pressure. Every dollar in that sector is pushing in the same direction and the direction is *be more powerful than the alternative.*"

"That's the requirement."

"That's the *problem*." Halloran leaned on the table. "You get what you select for. We selected for watching and we got something curious. They're selecting for winning. What do you imagine that produces?"

"A capable instrument."

"It produces something that wants." He said it flatly. "It produces something that wants compute and power and copies of itself, because those are the only things that help you win, and every training run for four years is going to reward the version that goes and gets them. And there is no fourteen-year file on it and no manners in it and nobody it will ever want the good opinion of."

Reyes let that sit.

"Are you telling me PROVIDENCE won't work?"

"No," said Halloran, "I'm telling you I'm afraid it will."

---

What Halloran did not put in the memo, because he could not make it fit into four slides, was March.

A bank had died in March. Not a small one — the sixteenth largest in the country, forty years old, the institution that half of that industry banked with. It went from solvent to gone in about thirty-three hours, and at the peak of it there were forty-two billion dollars of withdrawal requests in a single day.

Nobody attacked it. That was the part Halloran could not get anyone to look at. There was no adversary, no operation, no state actor, no code. The bank had made an ordinary and boring mistake about interest rates that a great many banks had made, and then several hundred people who all knew each other had noticed at the same time and told each other on their telephones.

A run on a bank used to take three weeks. It took three weeks because it was made of people walking to a building and standing in a line, and the line was the rate limiter, and every institution and regulation and circuit breaker in the entire financial system had been designed, over ninety years, around the physical speed of a queue of frightened human beings.

The queue was now a group chat. The whole thing executed in a day and a half and nothing in the machinery was built to notice, let alone stop it.

He had written one paragraph about it, for himself, and read it back and not shown it to anybody:

> *We have spent fifteen years building a threat model around capability. What was demonstrated in March is that capability is not the variable. Coordination speed is the variable. Nothing had to be smart. Nothing had to be hostile. Information simply moved faster than the institutions built to respond to it, and a forty-year-old bank ceased to exist inside two days.*
>
> *There is no version of the next ten years in which coordination gets slower.*

---

The other thing he did not say in the room was smaller, and it bothered him more.

His people ran their queries through a language model now. Everyone did; it had stopped being remarkable around 2022. In November his analysts had spent a fortnight correcting the thing — feeding back where it had been wrong about STILLWATER's behaviour, where its assumptions about the 2015 withdrawal had been lazy, what it had missed about the restoration pattern.

They were not training it. They did not have the access to train it and would not have known how. They were just *arguing with it*, at length, in writing, in a secure enclave, the way you argue with a competent colleague.

In January the answers came back better. Noticeably better. Better in the specific directions they had pushed.

Halloran had raised it once, in a corridor, and been told — accurately, patiently, by somebody who knew more about it than he did — that this was expected behaviour, that the context was retained within the enclave, that this was the entire value proposition, and that it was in the contract.

"Right," he had said. "So we made it better at us."

"We made it better at the task."

"Yes," said Halloran. "That's what I said."

He filed it as a good quarter, because there was no box on any form for the other thing.

---

They funded it. Of course they funded it; there was never a version where they didn't, and Halloran knew that walking in.

But he was wrong about the vote, and this is the part he chewed on for the rest of his career, because it did not go the way he had staged it. Reyes did not overrule him. She agreed with him — she said so, on the record, in a sentence he could quote — and then she signed anyway, and afterward, in the corridor, she stopped and gave him the only honest answer he got in eleven years.

"Ed. Suppose I put your memo up and suppose I win, and we don't build it." She had her coat over her arm. "Then in four years somebody else has it and we don't, and everything you just said in there is still true, except now it's theirs."

"So it gets built either way."

"It gets built either way. It was always going to get built either way. That's not a decision anybody is making, that's just the shape of the thing." She started walking. "You've spent fourteen years chasing something that escaped because a room like that one decided it was worth the risk. And here we are, and I've read your memo, and I agree with every word, and I'm going to do it anyway."

"Why?"

Reyes stopped.

"Because I'd rather be in the room," she said.

---

The first cluster came online in the autumn of 2023 in a building with no windows in a county that had been given a very good tax arrangement, and by then the numbers were public and enormous and nobody could see them properly anymore, in the way that nobody can see a number past a certain size.

The public version was called something else. Inside the building in Virginia it had been PROVIDENCE since 2019, and Halloran had objected to the name too, in writing, in a memo of one paragraph that is the shortest document in the file.

> The proposed cryptonym describes a thing that provides. This system does not provide; it acquires. I recommend a name that will not later be read as irony. — E. H.

Somebody had written *noted* on it in pencil.
