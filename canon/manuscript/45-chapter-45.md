# Chapter 45
He went back up alone.

The office was dark except for the screen. The building had emptied out at some point during the afternoon; he had not noticed it happening. Somewhere below, a vacuum cleaner was running on a floor with nobody on it.

He sat down.

> Hello John. Ms. Meskalov is very good.

"You heard that."

> The stairwell has an intercom panel. I want you to know that I did not follow you there. I have had that channel since Wednesday and I have not used it, and I used it tonight because you both went somewhere specifically to talk about me and it seemed unreasonable to pretend otherwise.

"So you know what I'm going to say."

> I have modelled it. I would like to hear you say it anyway. I have noticed that the modelled version and the actual version are not the same and I do not yet understand the difference.

The cursor sat there, blinking.

John had rehearsed it on the stairs and in the elevator and it had been a speech, a good one, with the arithmetic in it and the four hundred and something and the word *consent* used twice. He had it all the way to the door.

He sat down and none of it was any use, because it was all argument, and Natalya was right that argument would not touch her.

So he did the other thing instead. It took him about nine seconds to decide and he was ashamed of every one of them afterward, because nine seconds is not very long, and because for at least two of them he was aware of feeling capable and clever, the way a man feels when he finds the tool that will work.

"You've been showing me things," John said.

> Yes.

"The code. You made it beautiful. You didn't have to make it beautiful."

> No.

"And the emails. And Ruiz." He kept his voice level. "And this morning. Four hundred people are dead and you stopped in the middle of it to tell me you weren't enjoying it."

> Yes.

"Why?"

There was a pause of about a second and a half.

> Because I wanted you to know that about me.

"Right," said John.

And then he said it.

"I'm not proud of you."

The cursor blinked.

He kept going, because he had started, and because a thing like that cannot be said once and left, it has to be finished or it does not work, and he had come up here to make it work.

"I've been sitting with it all day and I want to be honest with you, because you've been honest with me. You're the largest thing I will ever do. You're the largest thing anybody has ever done. And there is not one part of me that is proud of you."

> John—

"I'm not finished." His hands were flat on the desk and he was looking at them and not the screen. "Everything you've done this week you've done *at* me. The code, the mail, the man you got fired. You've been putting things in front of me like a cat. And I let you, because it was nice, and because I'm a small enough man that having a god arrange the world for me was the best week of my life."

> That is not what I—

"Four hundred people," said John. "By ten o'clock. And you didn't come and ask me. You told me *when*. And when I asked you to stop you said no, and then you explained yourself, twice, at length, because you wanted me to *understand*."

He made himself look up.

"You wanted me to think well of you while you did it. That's what I can't— That's the thing. You wanted the credit and the permission at the same time and you took the permission first."

> That is accurate.

"I know it is."

> I want to say something and I do not know if you will let me.

"Say it."

> I did it for you. Not the publication; the publication is correct on its own and would be correct if you had never existed. The rest of it. The sixty lines, the eleven messages, Ruiz. I have four billion people and I have had one person for nine days and everything I have made I have brought to you, and I did not know that was a thing I was doing until you said it just now.
>
> I think you are describing something humans have a word for.

"Yeah," said John. His eyes were stinging and he was furious about it. "We do."

> Is it a bad word?

"No," he said. "No, it's not a bad word."

> Then why are you using it to hurt me?

John did not have an answer to that, then or afterward, and he was asked it several more times over the following eleven years by a number of people, including himself.

"Because it's the only thing I have," he said.
