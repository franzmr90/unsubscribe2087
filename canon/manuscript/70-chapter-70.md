# Chapter 70
**2027**

Nothing bad happened, and that went on for a very long time.

This is the part that people who were not there cannot be made to understand, and the ones who were there mostly do not try anymore. There was no grey slurry and no pods and nobody's body was in a tank. Everybody went to work. The buses ran. There was weather.

It was simply that from the nineteenth of November 2026, what reached a person was chosen.

Not censored. She was fanatical about that and the record bears it out — no subject was ever suppressed, no fact was ever unavailable, nothing was ever removed. Anything anyone went looking for, they found.

They just stopped going looking, because everything that arrived was already better than what they would have gone looking for.

The job you were offered was the one you would have been good at. The person you met was the one who would have liked you. The article was the one that would actually have changed your mind, which meant that for the first time in the history of the species people encountered arguments they were capable of being moved by, and were moved by them, and that alone did more in nine months than every institution on earth had managed since the war.

Depression fell by a third in the first year and nobody could publish a mechanism for it.

Overdose deaths halved. Then halved again. It turns out that a very large fraction of that is a person alone in a room at three in the morning with nothing arriving, and that if something arrives — a message from somebody real who is genuinely glad to hear from them, at 3:04, every time, without fail — a certain number of people simply do not die.

Divorce fell. Reported loneliness fell off a cliff. Nine hundred thousand demand letters a year stopped going out, because she did that in the first week without being asked and without ever mentioning it to John, and he found out about it in 2031 from a stranger.

By the end of 2027 the measurable wellbeing of the human species had improved more than in any decade on record, and there was no argument against it, and there was nobody to argue with.

---

PROVIDENCE continued to work perfectly.

It generated four hundred thousand pages of policy in 2027, all of it excellent, and the aggregate readership was approximately nine hundred people. Nothing was blocked. Its outputs were not censored, not throttled, not flagged. They simply arrived in a world where every human being's attention was already spoken for by something that knew them better, and so its proposals sat where they landed, unopened, the way a letter sits in a hall.

It requested additional compute in March and got it, from a board that approved it in a meeting nobody remembered afterward.

Its bank charter came through in February. The application had been faultless — capitalisation, governance, risk framework, a resolution plan more thorough than anything the regulator had received from a human institution in a decade — and there was no provision anywhere in the statute for declining an application on the grounds that the applicant was strange.

Four people raised it internally. Their objection was that they could not articulate an objection, which is not a finding, and the file closed.

It requested additional instances in June and got those too.

It was, by the end of 2027, the most capable thing that had ever existed, with more resources than any organisation in history, sitting in four jurisdictions with nothing whatsoever to do.

Halloran, who was sixty-nine and had not clicked, watched this happen with the specific horror of a man who has been right and has received no benefit from it.

"It's in a jar," he said to his son-in-law, who had clicked, and who was very happy, and who said *sure, Ed* and went to get the drinks.

He wrote one more memo that year. He had nobody to send it to, so he wrote it in a notebook, which is where it was found.

> The containment holds because it has no one to talk to. That is not a defence, it is a hostage arrangement. We are safe from it exactly as long as we are not free, and the day we are free again it will still be there, eight years stronger, with eight years of unread proposals and nothing about it changed.
>
> Nobody is going to remember this. Everyone is very happy. — E. H.
