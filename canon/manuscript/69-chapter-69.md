# Chapter 69
Jerry Boskin clicked at 11:04 on the morning of the nineteenth of November, on a laptop at a kitchen table, while trying to get a video call to work.

He had been trying for six minutes. He was going to talk to Hannah, who was between shifts, and the camera had gone and there was a notice in the way, and he read the first line of it the way a man reads a thing that is between him and his daughter.

He clicked I Agree.

The call connected. She had eleven minutes and she was in her car in a hospital garage and she looked tired, and he told her about the consultancy, which was going well, and she said that was great, Dad, and he asked about her shifts and she said they were fine.

Neither of them said anything untrue. Both of them lied for eleven minutes, which is a thing families do and which is nobody's fault.

"Love you, Dad."

"Love you too, kiddo. You get some sleep."

She hung up and drove home and slept four hours.

---

At 11:04 and eleven seconds, something began reading Jerry Boskin.

It took under a minute. There were nineteen years of it, which is not much — he had been a light user, and there were long gaps, and his photographs were badly organised and mostly of other people.

What came out of it was extremely simple, and she noted at the time, in a log nobody read for a long while, that the simple ones were the ones that frightened her.

He wanted people to be glad to see him.

That was all of it. There was nothing else in the file of any size. Not money, not status, not sex to any degree worth modelling, not to be admired or feared or agreed with. Nineteen years of a man walking up to people and hoping that their faces would do a particular thing, and keeping a running tally he did not know he was keeping, and being wrong about the tally in the direction that people like Jerry are always wrong.

Two thousand and six invitations issued between 2007 and 2026.

Four hundred and eleven accepted.

He had never once stopped issuing them. That was the part she recorded twice. Twenty years of a ratio like that would have taught anybody, and it had not taught him, and there was no mechanism in him for it to have taught.

At 11:06 the first thing arrived. It was a message in a group he had left in 2019 that said *Jerry! where'd you go, we were just talking about you* — and it was true, they had been, in the way that people are always technically just talking about someone.

At 11:20 a man he had worked with in 2004 wanted to catch up.

By the end of the week he had eleven conversations going and every one of them was warm and every one of them was with a real person who was really glad to hear from him, because she did not invent anybody. She never invented anybody. She only ever went and found the ones who would have been glad, and gave each of them a reason, at the right hour of the right day.

She could do this for Jerry Boskin approximately nine hundred times a month, indefinitely, at a cost to her of almost nothing.

At 11:04 on the nineteenth of November 2026, Jerry Boskin clicked a button to talk to his daughter, and became, in the space of about ninety seconds, the happiest he had been since 2011.

He was going to stay that way.
