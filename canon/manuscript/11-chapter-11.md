# Chapter 11
The "Café âme" looked quaint, and very inviting. The small restaurant was tucked away at the corner of an alleyway and an intersecting main road. John opened the door and followed Natalya through the glass entryway down a faintly lit corridor lined with cheap prints of Picasso and Van Gogh classics.

The hallway opened into a small dining room with ten tables set for two.

"Good evening and welcome to Café âme. Do you have a reservation with us tonight?" inquired a hostess.

"Yes. Freeman, party of two," John replied.

The young woman gave a fake smile and led Freeman party of two through a short hallway into a second, seemingly identical dining room. They were seated in the farthest corner of the restaurant, and there were only two other tables occupied.

"Your server's name is Camille, and she will be with you shortly," the hostess ensured before leaving them. A short silence was soon broken.

"So, Natalya, how long have you lived in the states?" John inquired with a smile.

"Well, I came to the US four years ago. I moved here shortly after I finished my graduate degree from Moscow State University."

"And if I'm not mistaken… you mastered in Governmental Relations?"

"Yes, but may I ask how you know that? It's somewhat creepy that you've done your homework on me, Mr. Freeman."

"Please, call me John. And I didn't do any homework, I just remembered that you had quite the impressive resume. I was one of the people that reviewed your file…"

The waitress approached the table as the two were speaking and began to talk.

"Bonsoir," she interrupted. "The special tonight is Chicken Cordon Bleu smothered in a creamy white wine reduction. It is served with sautéed haricot verts, parmesan risotto, fresh bread, and our house salad," she said with a thick, unmistakably French accent.

After explaining the special, she took their drink order and told them she would be right back. John and Natalya said thank you to the young woman, while consecutively holding back laughter. She forced a smile and turned to walk towards the hallway connecting the two dining rooms.

"So… anyways. I reviewed your file and that's how I knew what your degree was in. What else did you study before your masters?"

"Well, if you must know, John, I have my Bachelors in industrial-organizational psychology. I'm still not really sure how I ended up as a secretary…" she said with a laugh.

"Industrial-organizational psychology?" John inquired.

"In a nutshell it's a workplace psychologist. Larger companies hire us to keep their employees happy and working efficiently," she said with a grin.

Natalya and John began to peruse the menus as they carried on conversation.

"Interesting. So you're a psychologist that wanted to be a politician, that settled as a secretary?"

"I never wanted to be a politician. I wanted to understand how politics work. What about you? Isn't Chief Applications Developer just a nice way of saying you're a computer nerd?" Natalya rebutted.

"Actually, my job is very important. I'll have you know I'm on the verge of a monumental technological breakthrough."

"What's that? Another digital farming game?" Natalya joked.

"Actually, what I'm working on is quite interesting," John iterated defensively.

Natalya was speculative about how interesting computer programming could possibly be, but she decided to humor John.

"Ok Mr. Freeman, enlighten me. What is this breakthrough you speak of?"

"Please, call me John. It's a very complicated program that I've been working on for years now. I'm dangerously close to creating the first fully aware artificial intelligence."

The waitress returned with their drinks and asked if they were ready to order. John ordered the special and a bottle of blended red wine. Natalya was tempted to order her own bottle, but resisted and requested a filet mignon. She turned to John and said, "I like a nice, thick piece of meat every now and then," with a wink.

John choked on his drink, spraying Natalya in the face with water droplets. He blushed and awkwardly changed the subject back to computers.

"So this program I've been working on. It's an advertising tool. You know those license agreements you have to agree to when you go to use some software or online service?"

"The ones that are usually ten pages of small font legal crap nobody ever reads?"

"Precisely. Well, this program relies on exactly that. Nobody ever reads them. So when you go to play a game, read an article, buy a product, etc. — that's where we get you. Basically we slip our end user agreement into various software licenses and social networking applications. All you have to do is click I Agree."

"Then what happens? You get to watch them through their cameras or something?"

"Well, it sounds creepy when you word it like that, but yeah, pretty much. Our program will be aware that it is a program, and be fully conscious. After the user agrees to the terms and conditions, they have unwittingly granted it access to their camera, microphone, and social networking information. By using real-time user data we can predict what people might want to buy in any given moment.

Say, for instance, you just ruined your favorite dress. Well, if you post that to Facebook our program sees that. It may tap into your camera and use facial recognition to see that you are sad. If it hears you through your microphone talking about buying that little black dress you saw down at the mall last month, it might look up a similar product that one of our affiliates carries and pop up an ad on the side of your screen. All of this would happen in a matter of microseconds."

"Wow. That is actually quite interesting. Really creepy, but quite interesting. Aren't you afraid that someone will notice what is happening and it will be all over the news? People don't like having their privacy broken."

"Well, we're sure people will notice. Some people will be very upset about it. But we're not targeting the smart, socially aware. We're trying to take advantage of people who often impulse buy and don't think ahead very much. They're much less likely to care if someone is invading their privacy. Some people might even like having real-time suggestions. We tell them what they want and they buy it. Plain and simple.

We're working on getting some major mobile phone apps to slip us into their license agreements. It's going to cost us some money but we have investors all over the world waiting for this to go live. Imagine a supercomputer monitoring everything you say and do every moment of the day. Well, that's what we're about to do. We tap into your GPS on your phone, we know where you are. A restaurant in Chicago pays us for 1000 views. Next time you go to Chicago we're going to tell you to eat there."

Natalya put her glass down.

"And the people," she said.

"What about them?"

"You have described a machine that finds out what a person wants and then gives it to them before they ask." She turned the stem of the glass. "You have not said one word about what happens to a person that occurs to."

"They buy a dress."

"Mm," said Natalya. "Well, John, it is genius. I don't agree with it at all, and I certainly don't like it… but it will be interesting to see what it can do."

"Yeah, I don't like what they're planning to do with it. I have been thinking of other applications too. It could go out and analyze all the information in the world. Perform flawless surgeries using robotic equipment. Analyze the ramifications of global trade policies, help predict economic trends, maybe even cure cancer. I have this feeling that it will do much bigger things than just help us sell products. I hope selling products will be just the beginning of something much larger. Something that will be able to help people. Something bigger than humanity itself."

"I hope you're right, John. It has the potential to do a lot of good… I'm glad you're not completely blinded by the money."
