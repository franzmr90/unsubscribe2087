# Chapter 12
**Friday, May 13th, 1960 (55 years prior)**

It was Beatus' tenth birthday.

His father was sitting at the living room table with his hands folded, waiting, because he had been home for eleven minutes and had not taken his coat off.

There was a cake. It was a good one, from the baker on the corner, with ten candles in it, and it had been collected that afternoon by somebody who was not his father and arranged on a plate by somebody who was not his father.

There were no presents. Beatus looked for them, the way a boy does — not obviously, just a sweep of the room on the way in, the corners, behind the chair.

There was an envelope.

Inside the envelope was a letter of acceptance and a receipt for the first year's tuition at a school four hundred kilometres away, beginning in September, which was a very great deal of money and which was, his father explained, a better thing than a present, because a present is finished and this was a life.

Beatus said thank you. He was a well-mannered child.

"You will not like it at first," said his father. "That is not relevant."

An affluent family ensured that Beatus was going to grow up to be a great businessman, just like his father. It had been ensured before he was born. It had been ensured in the way that the weather is ensured, and nobody in that house had ever once used the word *want* about a child, because a child's wanting is not information, it is weather too, and it passes.

His father put out the candles himself, with his fingers, one at a time, because the boy had not thought to blow them out and the wax was going onto the icing.

Then he put a hand on his son's shoulder, which he did perhaps four times in his life, and said the only thing anybody in that room ever said to him about the next sixty-five years.

"God has a plan for you, son. Don't fuck this up."

Beatus Benedikt Fassbinder was ten years old and he had been given everything, and nothing had cost him anything, and it never would, and this is the whole of what was wrong with him and it took four minutes.
