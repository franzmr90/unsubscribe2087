# Chapter 4
John was irked by the way that Beats presented the project. As head of the programming department actually doing the work, he felt like he deserved more say in the presentations.

Beats had put the umlaut in it. That was the part. Phönix was a logo — it was for boards and for investors and for the side of a building. The thing on John's machine was called Phonix, with no decoration on it at all, because that is what you call something when you are going to be typing it four hundred times a day for three years.

Beats didn't really even explain how the program was to work, and that it was to be the first "fully conscious" computer program. It would be aware of itself and know that it was merely a program, but could learn about the world and how it works. It could help understand humans better; maybe even help us invent a cure for cancer.

It would have a profound impact on humanity as we know it. Not just as a data miner, but as an informational being… the first of its kind. It would be able to process information billions of times faster than the human brain, and have access to almost any information in the world.

Essentially it would be universal consciousness. The ultimate encyclopedia of human thoughts. Omnipotent and omniscient.

Two floors down, a man was already writing the sentence that would end all of it, on a Tuesday in June of 2011, and had forgotten it completely.
