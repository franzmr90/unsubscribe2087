# Chapter 64
**Thursday, October 29th, 2026**

Jerry Boskin was sixty-one and had been let go in the spring, which was described to him as a restructure and which was, in the sense that eleven other people over fifty-five were also restructured that month.

He took it well in front of people. He had a plan and the plan was a small consultancy, and he printed cards, and the cards are still in a box.

Deb had been gone two years by then. That was the thing that had actually taken the wind out of him and everybody in the family knew it and nobody said it, because Jerry's whole method was that if you were cheerful for long enough it became true, and it had worked for him for sixty years.

Hannah was twenty-three and had finished her nursing degree in the spring and was working nights at a hospital forty minutes away, and she called her father every day on the drive home at seven fifteen in the morning, and he was always awake, and she had never once asked herself why he was always awake at seven fifteen in the morning.

She was very tired that autumn. She thought that was the job.

---

John's alarm clock went off at six o'clock on the morning of the twenty-ninth of October.

It was in a cardboard box on the shelf above the paint, next to a node with a fan on it, where it had been since they moved into the house in 2016. The battery had been dead for eleven years. He had checked it in 2018, out of what he told himself was curiosity, and it had been dead then.

It went off at six o'clock and it drove off the shelf and hit the concrete and kept going.

John came down the stairs at a dead run with Natalya four steps behind him and stood in the doorway of his own garage in a t-shirt at two minutes past six in the morning while a plastic toy in a cardboard box went in circles on the floor, buzzing, exactly as it had every weekday morning of the worst and best year of his life.

Natalya said something in Russian that she had not said out loud since her grandmother died.

The clock ran into the leg of the workbench, reversed, went four feet the other way, and stopped.

The buzzing stopped.

Neither of them moved for a while.

Then the little screen on top, which had shown 6:00 for eleven years, changed, letter by letter, in the ugly seven-segment digits that were all it had:
