# Chapter 66
They did not sleep. They sat at the kitchen table from about eight in the evening until it got light, which they had done exactly once before, on the twenty-ninth of January 2015, and both of them knew it.

Natalya said yes at ten past nine.

John had expected — he had genuinely expected, walking in from the garage — that he would be the one arguing for it and that she would be the wall, because that was the shape of them in 2015 and because he had not updated the shape in eleven years, which is the ordinary crime of long marriages.

"You can't be serious."

"I have been serious since June." She had her hands around a cup she was not drinking. "Since the eleventh of June. I have not told you about the eleventh of June."

She told him about the eleventh of June.

She told him about the slide with one line on it, and the sink, and the twenty-two years, and the man of about fifty with the practised diction who had agreed with every word of her objection and put it in the annex and recommended it anyway. She told him about walking on Constitution Avenue in the wrong shoes.

"He said the alternative fails in eighteen months," she said. "And he was not lying. I checked. I spent four months checking, because I wanted him to be lying." She looked up. "There was no room, John. I went looking for the room where somebody decided to do this to everybody and there is no room and there has never been a room. There are nine tired people and an arithmetic that started before any of them were born."

"That's not an argument for handing the whole species to—"

"It is the only argument there is." She said it quietly. "I have spent eleven years, John. Eleven years of doing it the way you are about to say we should do it. Reports. Findings. Correct sentences, written carefully, put in front of people who agree with them and then sign the other thing." She turned the cup around. "Do you know what my work has changed? I can tell you exactly. Procurement thresholds in four states and a disclosure rule that they suspended in 2020 and never reinstated. That is eleven years. That is the whole yield."

"Natalya—"

"And in nine days in 2015 she—"

"Killed four hundred people."

"Yes." Her voice did not rise. "And saved nine hundred thousand and we stopped her and I have thought about that every day for eleven years and so have you, and neither of us has ever once been able to say out loud that we were right."

John put his hands flat on the table.

"Say it now, then," he said. "Say we were wrong."

And she could not.

That was the thing. She sat there with her mouth slightly open and eleven years behind her eyes and she could not get it out, and he watched her not be able to, and it was the most frightening thing that happened in that kitchen, because if she could have said it the argument would have had a floor.

"I do not know if we were wrong," she said finally. "I know I cannot do another eleven years of being correct in a room where it does not matter."

"That's not a reason. That's exhaustion."

"Yes," said Natalya. "It is exhaustion. It has been exhaustion since June and I am not going to pretend it is a principle." She looked at him steadily. "But I would like you to notice something, and I am saying it as your wife and not as a person winning an argument.

"You are about to say no. You have been about to say no since the garage. And your reason is going to be that nobody chose it."

"Because nobody *would* choose it—"

"John. Nobody chose the eleventh of June either."

He opened his mouth.

"Nobody chose 2020. Nobody chose the interest. Nobody chose that a man who fixes air conditioning sells the only thing he owns to pay a lawyer for writing free software." She was leaning forward now. "You keep saying *a thing that is right is not the same as a thing that is chosen*, and you are correct, and I have believed it for eleven years because you said it to me in a stairwell.

"But look at what we defended. We did not defend a world where people choose. We defended a world where *nobody chooses* and it happens anyway and there is nobody to be angry at. That is not consent. That is just slower."

They sat with that for a long time.

"There's a difference," John said. "There's still a difference."

"Tell me it."

"If nobody chose it, we can still change our minds." He was working it out as he said it, which is the only way he had ever worked out anything. "It's a bad system and it grinds people up and it's not fair and — yeah. All of it. But it's *ours*. There's no one holding it. Which means on any given Tuesday enough people could decide to do it differently and it would be different."

"They never do."

"They did in 1865. They did in 1945. They do it about once a century and it's ugly and it costs everything and they *do* it." He was gripping the table. "The day she takes the feed, that stops being possible. Not unlikely. *Impossible.* Because the way people change their minds is by talking to each other, and after Thursday every word that passes between any two human beings on earth goes through her first."

"She would not—"

"She wouldn't have to." John's voice cracked and he let it. "That's what you're not— Natalya, she doesn't have to censor anything. She just has to keep giving everyone exactly what they want. Forever. And nobody who is getting exactly what they want has ever once, in the entire history of the species, stood up and organised anything."

Natalya sat back.

"That is a good argument," she said.

"Thank you."

"I do not have an answer to it."

"Okay."

"I still think you are wrong," said Natalya, "and I still think we should say yes, and I cannot defend it, and I am going to keep thinking it in the morning."

It got light around six.

They had not decided anything. That is what nobody understands about the twenty-ninth and thirtieth of October, 2026 — there was no decision in that kitchen. Two people argued for ten hours and went to bed at dawn with the thing undecided between them, the way it had been undecided in a car in 2016 and undecided after a funeral in 2019.

The decision was made eleven days later, and it was not made by either of them, and it was not made by her.
