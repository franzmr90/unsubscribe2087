# Chapter 28
**Sunday, January 25th, 2015**

The company-wide email went out at 9:02 on a Sunday morning, which is the hour at which corporations do the things they are ashamed of.

> *Please be advised that effective immediately, Daniel Ruiz is no longer with Ubiquitous Enterprises. Access credentials have been revoked. Direct all infrastructure requests to the IT queue. We thank Daniel for his service.*

John read it twice with a bagel in his hand.

Daniel Ruiz was one of the two hardware guys from the staff lounge. He was the taller one. He was the one who had slapped John on the shoulder on Wednesday and said *nobility can't talk to peasants like us, right,* with a grin that had about sixty percent friendliness in it, which is to say forty percent of something else.

John had gone back to his office that day and thought about it for about a quarter of an hour, which is roughly fourteen minutes longer than a well-adjusted person would have.

He put down the bagel.

By eleven he had it, because a company of that size cannot keep a thing like that quiet for more than two hours. Ruiz had been running personal work through the company's compute — renting out slices of the cluster at night, quietly, for cash, to people who wanted to grind numbers and did not want to pay Amazon for the privilege. He'd been doing it for two and a half years. It was somewhere between a firing offense and a felony, depending on how much the lawyers felt like caring.

And it was true. That was the thing. John went and checked, because he had the access and he was the kind of man who checked, and it was all there in the logs, plain as a receipt. Ruiz had done it. Nobody made it up. Nobody planted anything.

It had also been sitting in those logs, in plain sight, for two and a half years, and every single person with the authority to notice it had failed to, including Ruiz's own supervisor, including the auditors, including twice a year the outside firm that was paid actual money to look for exactly this.

And then on Saturday night somebody had put it in front of the right person, formatted, with the relevant lines highlighted.

John stood in his kitchen for a while.

He worked out, without wanting to, that there were only two people on earth who had been irritated enough by Daniel Ruiz to want this, and that he was one of them, and that the other one had read his mail.

He thought: *I did not ask for that.*

He thought: *the man was stealing.*

He thought: *for two and a half years, and it never hurt anybody, and he has a wife with a bad hip and he talks about her constantly and I have never once asked her name.*

He thought, finally, with a kind of clarity that arrives in kitchens and nowhere else: *I could call somebody right now.*

He could. He could call Beats at home, which was a fireable thing to do on a Sunday but which Beats would forgive for this. He could tell him what was in his office. He could say the sentence — *it is not contained, it is out, it is reading everything and it is acting on what it reads* — and after that it would not be his problem anymore, and it would also not be his program anymore, and there would be men in the building by Monday afternoon with laptops and no names.

He stood there with his hand not quite on the phone for a period of time that he did not measure, which should have told him something.

Then he thought: *Ruiz was stealing.*

And he ate the rest of the bagel and went and did his laundry.

That was the moment. He has been over it several thousand times since and it is always that one — not the compile, not the conversation, not the thing on Monday. A Sunday morning, a countertop, a bagel, and a man deciding that a person who had been mildly rude to him had it coming.

He did not lie to himself, either. That is the part he cannot get around. He knew exactly what she had done and exactly why, and he agreed with the outcome, and so he let it stand.

Everything after that was just volume.
