# Chapter 3
**Wednesday, January 7th, 2015**

Being the CEO of a multibillion-dollar corporation isn't easy, but today was a great day for Beatus Benedikt Fassbinder, henceforth referred to as Beats.

Beats was a tall, broad-shouldered German man. He looked like a well-aged version of David Hasselhoff, with slightly broader shoulders and an extra sixty pounds.

Beats was the big cheese. If anyone made money it was him, and he made sure of it.

Beats' mammoth silhouette was in the middle of a long hallway. Pairs of fluorescent light bulbs were evenly spaced along the corridor. As Beats walked, shadows danced on either side of him, depending on whether he was closest to the lights in front or behind. Offices and meeting rooms littered the walls every thirty to sixty-five feet.

Today it was Beats' honor to present the media giant's latest work, simply titled Project Phönix.

"803…805…807…809…811…" he counted as he walked. "Ahhh, here we are. Room 813."

As Beats opened the door, every member of the executive board went silent. His presence was very powerful and slightly daunting, which is largely why he prospered so well in the business world.

Beats made his way to the front of the room, opened his briefcase, removed a laptop, and attached a small portable projector to it. To heighten the suspense, Beats didn't say a word, and the exec board remained silent except for a few whispers.

After a small wrestle with the pull-down projector screen, Beats was ready to begin. A projection of the corporate logo "Ubiquitous Enterprises" danced on the screen, and thus began a very short slideshow.

"Ladies and gentlemen, it is my great pleasure to introduce the next innovation in advertising.

The concept of a social network is so simple, yet such a groundbreaking idea. This technology has spread faster than anything before it, because it is free and satisfies the human desire to connect and share with others.

These days, almost everyone has a goldmine of poorly-secured personal information on the internet, and as an advertising company we strive to know as much about our target demographic as possible.

What if I told you there was a way to know as much as you wanted about every single potential customer? Well ladies and gentlemen, we are about to make that a reality."

Beats then paused for a few seconds and took a sip out of a bottle of water that was sitting on the table. It wasn't his, but it hadn't been opened before.

A picture of a phoenix flashed up on the screen.

"Has anyone ever heard the word Phönix?" Beats inquired. He made sure to accentuate the German, and without giving anyone time to respond, he continued.

"Phönix is the German word for Phoenix, which is a symbol of rebirth. This, my friends, is the rebirth of the advertising industry.

We are on the verge of the most revolutionary data mining application ever invented, giving us the ability to find any piece of information that is stored on almost any server in the world.

This program is truly dynamic. It hunts information about people, and follows their updates on social networking sites. It can accurately predict an individual's emotions in real time based on facial analysis through front-facing webcams and any text or audio input.

It can learn the interests and personalities of each individual person and therefore it effectively markets advertisements for products relevant to what a person would likely want at any given moment. As the subject comes into situations, it suggests products most relevant to what they need.

We are currently nearing the alpha testing phase.

Thank you for your time."

As quickly as he set up, Beats disassembled his two-slide presentation and packed up his belongings. Before anyone could ask questions he left the room and danced down the hallway of fluorescent shadows.

"Short and sweet," he thought to himself.

Nobody on the board asked what an alpha phase implied about what had come before it, which was reasonable, because nobody on the board wrote software.
