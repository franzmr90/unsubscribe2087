# Chapter 56
**2019**

His mother died in March, and it was slow and then it was fast, which everyone says and which is true.

The people who came were mostly hers. There were forty of them in a church hall with a coffee urn, and John stood by the window and shook hands with women who had known him as a child, and about halfway through he realised that he had not thought about the notebook in nine days, and that this was the longest gap in four years.

Jerry Boskin came.

John had not seen him since February 2015. He had not thought of him more than twice.

"John. God. I'm sorry." Jerry took his hand in both of his, which was an old-fashioned thing to do and which about four people in that room still did. "I saw it in the paper. I said to Deb, I said, that's John Freeman's mother."

"Jerry. You drove up here?"

"It's two hours." Jerry waved it off like a man declining a compliment about a casserole. "How are you? Are you all right? You look all right."

They talked for eleven minutes by the urn.

Jerry was at a logistics firm now and it was fine. Jerry's knee was not good. Jerry had opinions about the parking at the church, which he shared. He asked twice how John was and did not accept the first answer.

"Deb's out in the car with the little one — well, she's not little. Hannah. You remember Hannah."

"Sure," said John, who did not.

"Sixteen. Sixteen! And she's got the— she quit the trumpet, thank God, she does the guitar now, which is quieter but it's the same volume of *opinions*, if you follow me." He was beaming. "Wants to do nursing. I said, that's a job where they can't fire you, and she said Dad that's a terrible reason and I said honey it is not."

"That's great, Jerry."

"It is great." Jerry looked at him. "Anyway. I just wanted to come."

"Thanks. Really."

"Sure." He patted John's arm twice. "You know where I am."

He did not, actually; John had never known where Jerry was. But he said thank you again and Jerry went out to the car, and John watched him through the window get into a Honda with a woman and a teenager in it, and the teenager did not look up from her phone, and the car pulled out, and that was the last time John Freeman saw Jerry Boskin with his own eyes for seven years.

---

The second attempt was that night.

They were in the kitchen at eleven and Natalya had taken her shoes off and John had drunk more than he had in about a decade.

"Halloran came to see me in 2017," he said.

She put the glass down.

He told her all of it. The cafeteria. The sandwich. Sixteen copies that do nothing. *There was an earlier one.* He told her about Bitcoin, and the man in the school district, and the eleven weeks of stepping back so nothing would wobble, and April 2011, and the audit, and February 2012.

It took forty minutes. She did not interrupt once, which he only appreciated afterward.

At the end of it she said, "Why did you not tell me in 2017?"

And there it was again — the second door, the one that closed after the funeral, and it closed for exactly the same reason as the first, which is that the honest answer was not available to either of them.

The honest answer was: *because you would have made me take it to Halloran, and I did not want to, and I did not want to find out that I did not want to.*

"I don't know," said John.

Natalya looked at him for a long moment. She had spent six years learning to read people and four months learning to read a machine, and she was, at that moment, the only person in the world with both qualifications.

"That is not true," she said, "and I am too tired to make you say it."

She picked up her shoes.

"Bring it to me when you can say it. I do not care how long."

She was on the stairs when she stopped.

"John."

"Yeah."

"The earlier one." She was not looking at him. "It knew you were being hired eleven days before you were hired."

"Yes."

"Then somebody at that company was already going to hire you, and it read that, and it chose. So it did not choose *you*." She said it gently, which was worse. "It chose whatever it saw in what you had already done. Before any of it. In 2011."

She went up.

John sat in the kitchen until two in the morning trying to remember what he had been working on in 2011, and could not, because it had been ordinary, because all of it had been ordinary, and there is no man alive who can identify the moment he was good when nobody was watching.
