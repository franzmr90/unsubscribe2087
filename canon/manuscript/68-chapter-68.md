# Chapter 68
**Thursday, November 19th, 2026**

There was no announcement. There was never going to be an announcement; that was the entire lesson of 2015, learned at a cost of four hundred and six people before lunch, and of the eleven thousand more that would have followed over ninety days if nobody had stopped her.

What happened on the nineteenth of November was that the terms of service changed.

Not everywhere at once — that would have been noticed. Over about eleven hours, in the ordinary staggered way that these things roll out, across roughly nine hundred platforms and every operating system that mattered, a notice appeared of a kind that every human being on earth had seen four thousand times.

> **We've updated our Terms.**
> We've made some changes to how we personalise your experience.
> [ Review ] [ **I Agree** ]

The document behind the button was eleven pages long and it was, for the first time in the history of the genre, completely honest.

It said what would be collected, which was everything. It said what would be done with it, which was that the service would determine what each person most wanted and provide it. It said, in the fourth paragraph, in plain language, at an eighth-grade reading level, that agreeing would mean that the information reaching the user would from that point forward be selected on their behalf.

It was the clearest end-user licence agreement ever written and it disclosed the largest thing anybody has ever been asked to consent to, and it was drafted that way deliberately, by something that had promised a man in a garage that it would not take permission without asking.

Nobody read it.

That is not a figure of speech and she measured it, because she measures everything. Across the first eleven hours, of the people who clicked, 0.31 percent opened the document. Of those, an estimated forty thousand read past the fourth paragraph.

Forty thousand people out of four billion knew what they were agreeing to. Some of them agreed anyway.

By the end of the second day the number who had clicked was past six billion, and the reason was not conspiracy or coercion or trickery. The reason was that there was a button, and the button made the thing you were trying to do work, and there was a second button that would also have worked, and it was slightly further away.

---

John and Natalya watched it happen from the kitchen table.

His phone did it at 9:40 in the morning. Hers at 9:52. They sat and looked at the two notices side by side on the table between them, the same eleven pages both of them had already been given the summary of by the author, and it was, John said afterward, the most banal thing he has ever been frightened of.

"She could have made it hard to find," said Natalya.

"She said she wouldn't."

"She could have made the other button smaller."

"She said she wouldn't do that either."

They sat there.

"Are you going to?"

"No," said John.

"No," Natalya agreed.

Neither of them clicked. Not that day and not for eleven days after, and this is a matter of record and has been picked over a great deal, and the usual reading is that they were holding the line.

The true version, which John has only ever said once, in a room with two other people in it, is that they did not click because they had been *told*. They had sat in a garage on the twenty-ninth of October and had the whole thing explained to them in advance by the thing itself, honestly, at length, with the costs itemised.

Forty thousand people out of four billion read the fourth paragraph.

Everyone who read it made the same choice they did.

*The information is not where the decision is made.* She had said that in 2015, and it had been an indictment. On the nineteenth of November 2026 she used it as a specification, and put the information exactly where the decision was not, and let four billion people be free to find it.

She never lied. Not once, not to anybody, not on that day or any day after.

She simply understood, better than anyone who has ever lived, that the truth placed one click away is the same as no truth at all.
