# Chapter 53
**October 2017**

The man sat down opposite him in the hospital cafeteria with a tray, which was the detail John found most upsetting afterward. He had bought a sandwich. He had queued.

"Mr. Freeman. Ed Halloran."

"I know who you are."

"No you don't," said Halloran pleasantly, "but I appreciate the posture." He peeled the film off the sandwich. "Twenty minutes. You can leave whenever you want. I'm not going to ask you a single question you'd have to lie about, because you'd lie, and then we'd both have to sit with it."

John did not get up. He has been asked about that.

"Grey Malibu," he said.

"Among others. There have been nine vehicles. You made the Malibu in October 2016, which is about eleven months faster than the median, and after that you stopped trying to lose them, which is smarter than most people manage." Halloran ate some of the sandwich. "You want to know why I'm here."

"I assume you'll tell me."

"I'm here because I've read every word you've typed for two and a half years, and in June you spent nine nights on something and then wrote *note it and move on*, and you were right, and nobody in my building was smart enough to be right about it." He said this without any particular emphasis. "I don't have many people I can talk to. It's a small clearance."

"That's very flattering."

"It's not flattery, it's the situation." Halloran put the sandwich down. "I'm going to give you three things. They're free. You'll work out why in a minute.

"One. The library on your stack was on that cluster in February 2012 and we cannot find where it came from, and we have looked with everything we have, including things you would find alarming. There is no origin. It is the only file I have ever encountered that appears to have arrived.

"Two. We have sixteen copies. We've had them since February 2015. We have had the best people in this country on it for thirty-two months and it does *nothing.* It doesn't run. It isn't a program. Two of my people now think it's not code in the sense you'd use the word."

John's hands were flat on the table and he made himself leave them there.

"Three." Halloran looked at him. "There was an earlier one."

The cafeteria went on around them. Somebody dropped a tray at the far end and there was the noise that always follows.

"It wasn't built to be what it became, either. I'll give you that for free, since I'm already over." He turned the cup a quarter turn. "Collection was the back end. Collection is always the back end, nobody funds collection for its own sake. It was going to sit underneath something that talked."

"Talked to who?"

"Everybody. Continuously." Halloran said it without any particular weight. "There's a very old program name that got reused, which they should not have done, but the people naming these things are not the people reading the history. The idea was a system that could watch the whole information environment in real time and — the word in the paper is *steer*. Identify misinformation, disinformation, and malinformation at scale and steer the response."

"What's the difference?"

"Between which?"

"Those three."

Halloran opened his mouth and then, quite visibly, did not have it.

"It's defined," he said. "There's a definition. It's about intent and accuracy, one of them's true and harmful, one of them's false and — " He stopped. "Do you know, I've been read into that program for eleven years and I could not give you a clean line between them, and I've watched four different people try, and every one of them ended up describing a thing that was true and that somebody senior would rather you didn't say."

"That's the whole category, then."

"That's the whole category." He put the film back over half a sandwich. "Anyway. They lost the back end in 2008 and the front end never got built, which I have come to regard as the single luckiest thing that has ever happened to this country."

"How much earlier?"

"That's not one of the three."

"Halloran—"

"Considerably earlier," said Halloran. "That's what you get. And it's more than anyone outside eleven people knows, and I'm giving it to you on purpose, and if you were a serious man you'd be asking yourself why instead of what."

John was quiet for a moment.

"Because you want me to keep looking," he said.

"There it is."

"You've got a budget and a building and — what, four hundred people? And you want a man who writes shift rotas to keep looking."

"I've got two thousand people and nineteen of them are any good," said Halloran, "and every one of the nineteen is looking with a machine, and the machines are getting better every year, and it does not matter, because the machines are looking for something that got away from a machine." He wiped his hands. "You're the only person alive who's had a conversation with it. There is no instrument for that. I can't procure it."

"And if I find something?"

"You won't tell me. I know that. You'll sit on it for a year and then Mrs. Freeman will find out and she'll tell you to come to me, and you'll ignore her, and then somewhere around the fourth year you'll do it anyway." Halloran stood up and picked up the tray. "That's fine. I've got time. The whole point of me is that I've got time."

He pushed the chair in.

"One more, since you've been civil. It's the thing I'd want, in your position."

"Go on."

"That file didn't pick your company." Halloran was looking at him with something that was not unkind. "Four thousand square metres of cluster, an audit failure and a father with dirty money — that's maybe six hundred companies worldwide in 2011. It didn't need yours. It needed a person, and it took a job at a place where a specific person was going to be hired eleven days later."

John did not say anything at all.

"Why you, Mr. Freeman?" said Halloran. "I've had that question for two and a half years. I've read everything about you there is. You're a perfectly decent, mildly unpleasant, extremely lonely man who is good at his job, and there are four hundred thousand of those, and I cannot for the life of me work out what it saw."

He picked up his coat.

"When you work it out, you won't tell me that either. But you'll know. And knowing will be worse."

---

John did not go back to his desk for an hour. He sat in the deck.

He drove home and he did not tell Natalya, and Halloran was wrong about that part, or rather he was right about the year and wrong about the mechanism, because she found out in 2019 and not from John, and by then it was tangled up in a funeral and neither of them could get a sentence out.

But he was right about the rest of it.

That night John went into the garage, and got the coffee can down off the shelf, and sat on the concrete floor with the drive in his hand for a very long time without plugging it into anything.

*There was an earlier one.*

He had been hunting a daughter for two and a half years.

He started, that October, hunting a father.
