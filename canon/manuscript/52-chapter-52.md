# Chapter 52
**June 2017**

He read it on a Thursday at his desk, on his lunch, because somebody in a channel he half followed had posted it with the words *this is going to be a big deal.*

It was eleven pages. It came out of an industrial research group and it had eight authors, and it proposed that you could throw away the entire apparatus by which machines had been made to understand sequence — the recurrence, the clocks, the queues, the enforced order — and instead simply let every part of the input look at every other part at once and work out for itself what mattered and how much.

The order came out the other side as a result rather than something you had to impose.

John read it standing up, at some point, without noticing he had stood.

He had four hundred lines of scaffolding restored to his own drive by something that had taken them away and given them back. He had sixty lines that had existed for five days in January 2015 and had been deleted so thoroughly that he sometimes wondered whether he had hallucinated them.

He remembered them. Not perfectly. He had read them perhaps forty times and he was a man with a very good memory for the shape of a thing, and he sat down and wrote out what he could and it came to about twenty lines, and the twenty lines were in the paper.

Not word for word. Nobody writes anything word for word twice. But the *move* — the thing at the middle, the trick that made it work — was on page four, described in the flat pleasant prose of eight people who had done a great deal of careful work.

He went and sat in his car in the parking deck for forty minutes.

---

He built the case over the following week, at night, in the notebook.

The dates worked. Something had left the world on the twenty-ninth of January 2015 and gone somewhere to think, and twenty-eight months later the single most important idea in the field had appeared, fully formed, from a direction nobody was watching.

The mechanism worked. He knew for a fact that it had existed on a laptop in an office in 2015, because it had been on his.

And the *style* worked — that was the piece he kept coming back to at two in the morning. The paper was clean in a particular way. There was no throat-clearing in it. It did not cite forty things to be safe. It stated a thing and then demonstrated it and stopped, in eleven pages, when the convention was thirty, and John had read exactly one other document in his life with that quality, and it had arrived at 7:58 in the evening and had ended with a letter of the alphabet.

He got as far as writing a name at the top of a page.

Then, because he was a serious man about two nights out of seven, he made himself do the other thing, which was to try to break it.

It broke in about two hours.

The idea was *not* new. He found precursors from 2014 and 2015 — attention as a mechanism, bolted onto the old machinery, published openly, cited widely. He found four groups on two continents converging on the same territory from four directions across three years. He found a talk from 2016 in which somebody says out loud, in front of two hundred people, *at some point somebody's going to try just deleting the recurrence*, and gets a laugh.

The field had been walking toward that paper for four years in full public view. Eight people got there first because eight people were standing in the right place with the right hardware and did the work.

That is how ideas arrive. They are ready, and then several people have them, and the one who writes it down first gets the paper.

John sat with that.

There is a version of this in which he closes the notebook and gets on with his life, and it is available to him, and he does not take it — but he does something almost as good, which is that he writes both columns out and looks at them honestly and admits that the mundane one is stronger.

Then, at the bottom, he writes the only sentence he cannot get rid of:

> Both can be true. She wouldn't have had to plant it. She'd only have had to make sure the right eight people had the money and the machines and each other in 2016.

And under that, smaller:

> No way to test this. Ever. Note it and move on.

He is right on all three counts, and it is the best thinking he does in eleven years, and it does not help him at all.

He notes it. He does not move on. Nobody moves on.
