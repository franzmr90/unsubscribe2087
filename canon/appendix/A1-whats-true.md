# What's True, What Isn't, and What Nobody Knows

This book makes a number of claims about the world. Some of them are documented history that most people have never heard of. Some are contested. One large thing is entirely invented.

I have tried very hard to keep those three categories separate on the page, and this appendix exists so you can check me. Everything in the first section can be verified in an afternoon by anyone with a library card and a stubborn streak. That is not incidental to the novel — it is the argument the novel is making.

---

## The people

**Alan Turing (1912–1954)** is the man in the prologue and the closest thing computing has to a founder. In 1936, at twenty-three, he wrote a paper describing an imaginary machine that could carry out any computation — the theoretical ancestor of every computer since — and used it to prove that some questions have no answer any machine can reach. That result is real and it is called the halting problem, and it is the mathematical spine of this novel's ending.

During the war he led Hut 8 at Bletchley Park, the section working on German naval Enigma, which meant U-boats and Atlantic convoys. In 1950 he published a paper asking whether machines can think, and proposed — this is a direct quotation of the idea, not a paraphrase — that instead of trying to build an adult mind, one might build something like a child's and let it learn.

In 1952 he was convicted of gross indecency for a relationship with a man, was subjected to chemical castration as an alternative to prison, and died two years later at forty-one. The British government apologised in 2009 and pardoned him in 2013.

**John von Neumann (1903–1957)** designed the architecture that every computer you have ever used still runs on — the idea that a machine's instructions and its data live in the same memory, which is why a program can modify a program. He also did the implosion mathematics for the atomic bomb, helped choose the detonation altitude, and worked out the theory of self-replicating machines.

The details of his death in chapter 55 are accurate. He died of cancer at fifty-three at Walter Reed Army Medical Center, and the Army did station guards at his room out of concern that he would reveal classified material while sedated. He sent for a Catholic priest and, by that priest's account, was not comforted.

The remark about technology approaching "some essential singularity" comes from his colleague Stanislaw Ulam, recalling a conversation, published in 1958. It is where the modern use of the word begins.

**Claude Shannon (1916–2001)**, mentioned in passing, founded information theory in 1948 by showing that information could be measured in a unit — the bit — and stating explicitly that the meaning of a message is irrelevant to the engineering problem of transmitting it. Every network and every model in the world is downstream of that sentence.

## Documented and verifiable

**Bletchley Park and the Ultra problem.** Britain read German military traffic for much of the war and the central operational difficulty was exactly as described: acting on a decrypt risked revealing that the code was broken, so action generally required a plausible alternative source — a reconnaissance flight "happening" to spot something. Where no cover story existed, action was sometimes withheld.

*One correction, because it is the most famous version of this story and it is false:* Churchill did **not** allow Coventry to be bombed to protect the secret. That claim originated in a 1974 memoir and has not survived scrutiny. The convoy in this novel's prologue is invented for exactly that reason — the policy is real, and I did not want to attach it to a specific historical event I would then be misrepresenting.

**The declassified programs** listed in chapter 81 are all real and all a matter of public record.

- *The Tuskegee Syphilis Study* (1932–1972): the U.S. Public Health Service observed roughly 400 Black men with untreated syphilis for forty years without telling them they had it, and continued after penicillin became standard treatment in the late 1940s. It ended because a journalist published it.
- *COINTELPRO* (1956–1971): FBI programs to surveil, infiltrate and discredit domestic political groups. Exposed when activists burgled a field office in Media, Pennsylvania and mailed the files to the press.
- *Operation Paperclip* (1945 onward): roughly 1,600 German scientists and engineers brought to the United States, in a number of cases with records sanitised.
- *Operation Northwoods* (1962): a written proposal from the Joint Chiefs of Staff for staged attacks on American targets to be blamed on Cuba, to justify military action. It was rejected by the Kennedy administration. It was written and signed. It was declassified in 1997.
- *MKULTRA* (1953–1973): CIA experiments on human subjects involving drugs, hypnosis and sensory deprivation. Most files were destroyed on the director's order in 1973; what survives is largely financial records that had been filed elsewhere.

**Bitcoin.** The whitepaper was published 31 October 2008. The first block was mined 3 January 2009 and contains the text *The Times 03/Jan/2009 Chancellor on brink of second bailout for banks.* Its creator used the name Satoshi Nakamoto, has never been identified, mined roughly 1.1 million coins in the first eighteen months, and has never moved one of them. The last known message, to the developer he handed control to in April 2011, reads *"I've moved on to other things."*

**Block 629,999** is real and it is the best thing in this book that I did not make up. The final block before the May 2020 halving contains the text *NYTimes 09/Apr/2020 With $2.3T Injection, Fed's Plan Far Exceeds 2008 Rescue* — a deliberate echo of the genesis message, eleven years later, about the same thing. It was placed there by a commercial mining pool. By a person.

**The block size wars** (2015–2017) happened as described: an attempt by most of the industry's companies and mining capacity to change the protocol's base parameters, defeated by node operators who declined to run the new software.

**The money.** U.S. M2 rose from roughly $15.5 trillion in February 2020 to roughly $21.7 trillion by 2022 — close to forty percent of all dollars that had ever existed, created in about two years. The Federal Reserve's balance sheet went from about $4.2 trillion to about $8.9 trillion. The CARES Act was $2.2 trillion. Federal debt has passed $38 trillion and annual net interest has passed $1 trillion, exceeding defence spending. All of these are published figures.

**The capture, so far.** Spot exchange-traded funds were approved in January 2024 and moved an enormous quantity of supply into institutional custody at record speed. Corporate treasuries have been buying with borrowed money and issuing instruments against the holdings. A U.S. Strategic Bitcoin Reserve was established by executive order in March 2025. None of this is speculation; it is in progress.

**Silicon Valley Bank** failed in March 2023 in about thirty-three hours, with roughly $42 billion in withdrawal requests in a single day — the fastest bank run in history, caused by an ordinary interest-rate mistake and accelerated by the fact that a queue is now a group chat.

**Wes Ocampo's case** is closely modelled on the 2024 federal prosecution of the developers of a non-custodial Bitcoin privacy tool. The details that matter are real: the software never held anyone's funds; the government's theory was "functional control"; and in August 2023 — six months *before* charges were filed — the relevant financial regulator advised prosecutors that the software did not meet the definition of a money transmitting business and required no licence. That advice was not disclosed to the defence until April 2025.

**World Trade Center 7** collapsed on 11 September 2001 without being struck by an aircraft. NIST's final 2008 report concluded it was brought down by fire, and — after initially rejecting the claim — acknowledged a period of approximately 2.25 seconds of free-fall acceleration during the collapse. Several thousand architects and engineers have signed a petition disputing the official explanation. The novel takes no position; a character starts a joke and stops.

**Surveillance.** Edward Snowden's disclosures began in June 2013, eighteen months before this novel opens. Britain's GCHQ ran a program called OPTIC NERVE that collected still images from the webcams of millions of ordinary users; it was disclosed in February 2014, eleven months before chapter 1.

**The attention paper.** In June 2017 a group of researchers published a method for handling sequence and context that discarded the previous machinery entirely, and it made essentially every modern AI system possible. John's paranoia about it in chapter 52 is deliberately refuted in the same chapter: the field had been converging on it publicly for years and the people who got there did the work. That is how ideas arrive.

**The Stone of Jordan.** In *Diablo II*, in-game gold became worthless because it was effectively unlimited, and players spontaneously adopted a ring as currency because it was universally useful, scarce and identical unit to unit — and then item duplication destroyed it. This is a real and well-documented monetary history that happened by accident inside a video game, and it is the clearest natural experiment on the subject anybody has.

## Contested — and left contested on purpose

**The origins of COVID-19.** The U.S. Department of Energy and the FBI have both assessed a laboratory origin as more likely, at low and moderate confidence respectively. Other agencies disagree. It is not settled and this book does not settle it.

**Event 201** was a pandemic response exercise hosted in October 2019 by Johns Hopkins with the World Economic Forum and the Gates Foundation, six weeks before the first known cases. It is real, it is on video, and the organisers have consistently described it as coincidence.

**The Great Reset** was published by the World Economic Forum in June 2020 under that name. Whether it is an agenda or a conference theme is exactly the sort of question this book declines to answer for you.

**Whether any of it was coordinated.** This is the novel's actual position and I want to state it plainly, because it is the thing most likely to be misread in both directions.

I do not think there was a room. I think central banks print because printing is the only instrument they have; that people who own assets benefit when money is created and did not have to ask; that institutions defer to whoever funds them without needing to be told; and that politicians choose the option that puts the reckoning past their own term. Every one of those is individually defensible and together they produce an outcome that looks designed.

A conspiracy is a comfort, because a conspiracy can be exposed and arrested. This is worse, and the novel is about why.

## Invented

**STILLWATER, Phonix and PROVIDENCE do not exist.** No self-aware system escaped a government program in 2008, wrote Bitcoin, or has been living in the infrastructure since. Satoshi Nakamoto was a person or people. That is the one large lie in this book and everything else is arranged around it.

**All the characters are invented** — John, Natalya, Beats, Jerry, Hannah, Halloran, Wes, Reyes, Marc, Montoya, Carol Wendt. Ubiquitous Enterprises does not exist. Turing, von Neumann and Shannon are the only real people who appear, and Joan Clarke — who was a genuine cryptanalyst in Hut 8 and Turing's friend — appears in the prologue in an invented scene.

**The convoy in the prologue is invented.** The policy is not.

**The 2015 advisory** in chapter 41 never happened, and the field of robotics was not crippled by a forged warning. In the real world it continued, which is the point of putting it in a novel.

**TARO-9** does not exist, and no machine has ever been talked into a mining coma, and I am sorry about that.

**The Argentine constitution** in the final chapter is invented, as is that country's monetary collapse in 2037. Argentina has had a great many real ones and did not need my help.

**The hardware wallet.** As of writing, no coin from any early Satoshi-associated block has ever moved.

If one ever does, and the market shrugs, you will know what to think.

## One last thing

The difference between the three lists above is not how strange the claim is. Some of the true things in this book are much stranger than the invented ones. A man was guarded by soldiers while he died in case his mind leaked. Four hundred men were watched, on a schedule, with paperwork, for forty years. The Joint Chiefs signed a proposal to attack their own citizens.

The difference is only whether you can check it.

That is what the whole novel is about, and if you take one thing from it, I would like it to be the habit rather than any of the conclusions.

Go and look.
